# Theorem: Generalized Holder inequality for Gamma zeta

## Statement

Let \(m\ge2\), \(r\ge1\), \(x_1,\ldots,x_m>1\), and \(p_1,\ldots,p_m>1\) satisfy
\[
\sum_{i=1}^m\frac1{p_i}=\frac1r.
\]
Define
\[
T=r\sum_{i=1}^m\frac{x_i}{p_i}.
\]
Then \(T>1\) and
\[
\zeta(T)\le
\frac{\prod_{i=1}^m(\Gamma(x_i)\zeta(x_i))^{r/p_i}}{\Gamma(T)}.
\]

## Proof

Let
\[
M(s)=\Gamma(s)\zeta(s)=\int_0^\infty\frac{t^{s-1}}{e^t-1}\,dt,
\qquad s>1.
\]
Set
\[
\lambda_i=\frac{r}{p_i}.
\]
Then \(\lambda_i>0\), \(\sum_i\lambda_i=1\), and \(T=\sum_i\lambda_i x_i>1\).

Define \(q_i=p_i/r\). Then \(\sum_i1/q_i=1\). The assumptions imply \(q_i>1\) for every \(i\): if \(q_j\le1\), then \(1/p_j\ge1/r\), leaving no room for the other positive terms in \(\sum_i1/p_i=1/r\).

For \(t>0\), define
\[
f_i(t)=\left(\frac{t^{x_i-1}}{e^t-1}\right)^{1/p_i}.
\]
Then
\[
\left(\prod_{i=1}^m f_i(t)\right)^r
=
\frac{t^{r\sum_i(x_i-1)/p_i}}{e^t-1}
=
\frac{t^{T-1}}{e^t-1},
\]
because \(r\sum_i1/p_i=1\). Therefore
\[
\left\|\prod_{i=1}^m f_i\right\|_{L^r(0,\infty)}^r
=
\int_0^\infty\frac{t^{T-1}}{e^t-1}\,dt
=\Gamma(T)\zeta(T).
\]
By generalized Holder,
\[
\left\|\prod_{i=1}^m f_i\right\|_{L^r}
\le
\prod_{i=1}^m\|f_i\|_{L^{p_i}}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\|f_i\|_{L^{p_i}}^{p_i}
=\int_0^\infty\frac{t^{x_i-1}}{e^t-1}\,dt
=\Gamma(x_i)\zeta(x_i).
\]
Thus
\[
(\Gamma(T)\zeta(T))^{1/r}
\le
\prod_{i=1}^m(\Gamma(x_i)\zeta(x_i))^{1/p_i}.
\]
Raising both sides to the power \(r\) and dividing by \(\Gamma(T)\) proves the claimed inequality.
