# Proposition: Low-support growing-prime criterion for Erdos 536

## Statement

Let \(P\) be a finite set of primes and use the notation of [[wiki/nodes/mrw-f835f9671070|Finite-prime weighted-grid reduction for Erdos 536]].  For
\[
\alpha=(\alpha_p)_{p\in P}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^P
\]
write
\[
\sigma_P(\alpha)=|\{p\in P:\alpha_p>0\}|,
\qquad
S_P=\sum_{p\in P}\frac1p.
\]
For \(0\le\theta<1\), define the low-support prefix count
\[
L_{P,\theta}(t)=
\#\left\{
\alpha\in\Gamma_P(t):
\sigma_P(\alpha)\le \theta S_P
\right\}.
\]
Define the high-support residual
\[
R_P(\theta)=
\delta_P\int_1^\infty
\bigl(g_P(t)-L_{P,\theta}(t)\bigr)_+\,t^{-2}\,dt,
\qquad
\delta_P=\prod_{p\in P}\left(1-\frac1p\right).
\]
This is a prefix-rank residual; it is not assumed to be the measure of one global extremal grid-bad-free set.

Suppose there are finite prime sets \(P_j\) and a fixed \(0\le\theta<1\) such that
\[
S_{P_j}\to\infty
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
R_{P_j}(\theta)\to0.
\]
Then Erdos Problem #536 has an affirmative density answer:
\[
f(N)=o(N).
\]

In particular, it is enough to prove this residual condition with \(L_{P,\theta}\) replaced by any smaller support envelope, such as the count of vectors with \(\sigma_P(\alpha)\le r\) for a fixed integer \(r\), provided the corresponding residual tends to \(0\) along a sequence with \(S_P\to\infty\).

## Proof

For a finite prime set \(P\), define a probability measure on \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{P}\) by
\[
\mu_P(\alpha)
=
\delta_P\prod_{p\in P}p^{-\alpha_p}.
\]
It is a probability measure because
\[
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{P}}
\prod_{p\in P}p^{-\alpha_p}
=
\prod_{p\in P}\left(1-\frac1p\right)^{-1}
=
\delta_P^{-1}.
\]
Under \(\mu_P\), the coordinates are independent and
\[
\mu_P(\alpha_p=k)
=
\left(1-\frac1p\right)p^{-k}
\qquad(k\ge0).
\]
Therefore the events \(\{\alpha_p>0\}\) are independent and have probabilities
\[
\mu_P(\alpha_p>0)
=
\sum_{k\ge1}\left(1-\frac1p\right)p^{-k}
=
\frac1p.
\]
Thus
\[
\sigma_P(\alpha)=\sum_{p\in P}1_{\alpha_p>0}
\]
is a sum of independent Bernoulli variables with mean \(S_P\).

The jump identity gives
\[
\delta_P\int_1^\infty L_{P,\theta}(t)t^{-2}\,dt
=
\delta_P
\sum_{\substack{\alpha\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{P}\\
\sigma_P(\alpha)\le\theta S_P}}
\prod_{p\in P}p^{-\alpha_p}
=
\mu_P(\sigma_P\le\theta S_P).
\]
The integrals are finite for fixed \(P\), since
\[
0\le L_{P,\theta}(t)\le |\Gamma_P(t)|,
\qquad
0\le (g_P(t)-L_{P,\theta}(t))_+\le g_P(t)\le|\Gamma_P(t)|,
\]
and
\[
|\Gamma_P(t)|=O_P((1+\log t)^{|P|}).
\]
For \(0\le\theta<1\), the elementary Chernoff bound for independent Bernoulli sums gives
\[
\mu_P(\sigma_P\le\theta S_P)
\le
\exp\left(-\frac{(1-\theta)^2S_P}{2}\right).
\]
For completeness, here is the short proof.  For \(\lambda>0\), Markov's inequality gives
\[
\mu_P(\sigma_P\le\theta S_P)
\le
\exp(\lambda\theta S_P)
\prod_{p\in P}\left(1-\frac1p+\frac1p e^{-\lambda}\right).
\]
Using \(1+x\le e^x\), this is at most
\[
\exp\left(\lambda\theta S_P+(e^{-\lambda}-1)S_P\right).
\]
For \(0<\theta<1\), choose \(e^{-\lambda}=\theta\).  The exponent becomes
\[
S_P(\theta-1-\theta\log\theta),
\]
which is at most \(-(1-\theta)^2S_P/2\).  The endpoint \(\theta=0\) follows by letting \(\lambda\to\infty\).

Since
\[
g_P(t)\le L_{P,\theta}(t)+\bigl(g_P(t)-L_{P,\theta}(t)\bigr)_+,
\]
we have
\[
\delta_P\int_1^\infty g_P(t)t^{-2}\,dt
\le
\mu_P(\sigma_P\le\theta S_P)+R_P(\theta).
\]
For \(P=P_j\), the right side tends to \(0\) by the hypotheses \(S_{P_j}\to\infty\) and \(R_{P_j}(\theta)\to0\).

Applying [[wiki/nodes/mrw-f835f9671070|Finite-prime weighted-grid reduction for Erdos 536]] to each fixed \(P_j\), for every \(j\) one has
\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{f(N)}N
\le
\delta_{P_j}\int_1^\infty g_{P_j}(t)t^{-2}\,dt.
\]
Now take \(j\to\infty\) after this fixed-\(P_j\) limsup bound.  This proves
\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{f(N)}N=0,
\]
which is equivalent to \(f(N)=o(N)\).

For the final bounded-support assertion, if \(L_{P,r}(t)\) counts vectors with \(\sigma_P(\alpha)\le r\), then for every fixed \(\theta<1\) and all sufficiently large \(j\), \(r\le\theta S_{P_j}\).  Hence \(L_{P_j,r}(t)\le L_{P_j,\theta}(t)\), so
\[
\bigl(g_{P_j}(t)-L_{P_j,\theta}(t)\bigr)_+
\le
\bigl(g_{P_j}(t)-L_{P_j,r}(t)\bigr)_+.
\]
Thus a residual bound relative to fixed support \(r\) implies the displayed low-support residual condition.

## Notes

- This proposition is a conditional bridge, not a proof that \(R_P(\theta)\to0\).
- The fixed-prime axis floor from [[wiki/nodes/mrw-2060f97aad60|Fixed finite-prime axis-floor obstruction for Erdos 536]] is exactly a very low-support phenomenon.  This proposition explains why such low-support mass is not a terminal obstruction once \(P\) grows and \(S_P\to\infty\).
- Quantifier warning: the proof uses the finite-prime reduction for each fixed \(P_j\), then lets \(j\to\infty\).  It does not justify choosing \(P=P(N)\) without a new uniform finite-sum-to-integral estimate.
- Axis-floor compatibility: since \(1/(p-1)\le 2/p\) for \(p\ge2\),
\[
\delta_P\left(1+\sum_{p\in P}\frac1{p-1}\right)
\le
e^{-S_P}(1+2S_P)\to0
\]
whenever \(S_P\to\infty\).  Thus the previous fixed-prime floor disappears in the growing-prime regime.
- The new progress invariant is \(R_P(\theta)\), the weighted rank-envelope mass above a strict lower tail of the harmonic support distribution.
- A future proof of \(R_P(\theta)\to0\) would need to show that grid-bad-free prefix rank cannot persist through typical harmonic support levels.  A future obstruction would be a construction or rank-envelope lower bound with nonvanishing harmonic mass at support \(\ge\theta S_P\) for every \(\theta<1\).
