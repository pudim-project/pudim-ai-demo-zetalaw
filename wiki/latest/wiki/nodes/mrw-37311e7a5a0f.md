# Theorem: Variational sharp threshold for polynomial Gamma-product monotonicity

## Statement

Let \(m\ge1\), let
\[
u_m(s)=s^m+1,\qquad
J_m(s)=\frac{u_m'(s)}{u_m(s)}
=\frac{m s^{m-1}}{s^m+1},
\qquad s\ge1,
\]
and let \(\Psi^{-1}\) denote the inverse of the strictly increasing digamma function
\[
\Psi(x)=\psi(x),\qquad x>0.
\]
Define
\[
R_m(s)=\Psi^{-1}\!\big(J_m(s)-\psi(s)\big)-s,\qquad s\ge1.
\]
Then \(R_m\) is continuous, \(R_m(s)\to-\infty\) as \(s\to\infty\), and hence
\[
\rho_m=\max_{s\ge1}R_m(s)
\]
exists.  For \(\rho>-1\), the function
\[
\Phi_{\rho,m}(s)=\frac{s^m+1}{\Gamma(s+\rho)\Gamma(s)}
\]
is strictly decreasing on \([1,\infty)\) if and only if
\[
\rho\ge \rho_m.
\]
Moreover, for \(m\ge4\) the sharp threshold is not given by the left endpoint \(s=1\): \(R_m'(1)>0\), so
\[
\rho_m>R_m(1).
\]
If \(s_m>1\) is an interior maximizer of \(R_m\), then the sharp pair \((s_m,\rho_m)\) satisfies
\[
\psi(s_m+\rho_m)=J_m(s_m)-\psi(s_m)
\]
and
\[
\psi'(s_m+\rho_m)=J_m'(s_m)-\psi'(s_m).
\]

## Proof

The digamma function is strictly increasing from \(-\infty\) to \(+\infty\) on \((0,\infty)\), so \(\Psi^{-1}\) is well-defined and continuous.  The logarithmic derivative of \(\Phi_{\rho,m}\) is
\[
L_{\rho,m}(s)
=\frac{d}{ds}\log \Phi_{\rho,m}(s)
=J_m(s)-\psi(s+\rho)-\psi(s).
\]
Thus \(\Phi_{\rho,m}\) is nonincreasing on \([1,\infty)\) exactly when
\[
\psi(s+\rho)\ge J_m(s)-\psi(s)
\qquad(s\ge1).
\]
Since \(\psi\) is strictly increasing, this pointwise condition is equivalent to
\[
s+\rho\ge \Psi^{-1}\!\big(J_m(s)-\psi(s)\big)
\qquad(s\ge1),
\]
or
\[
\rho\ge R_m(s)\qquad(s\ge1).
\]
Therefore the exact nonincreasing threshold is \(\rho_m=\sup_{s\ge1}R_m(s)\), provided the supremum is finite.  At and above this threshold, the function is in fact strictly decreasing.  Indeed, its logarithmic derivative is analytic on \((1,\infty)\); if it vanished on an interval, analytic continuation would force
\[
J_m(s)=\psi(s+\rho)+\psi(s)
\]
identically on a half-line, contradicting the asymptotics \(J_m(s)=O(1/s)\) and \(\psi(s+\rho)+\psi(s)=2\log s+o(\log s)\).  Hence the logarithmic derivative is not identically zero on any interval, and the integral of it over every nondegenerate interval is strictly negative.

It remains to check compactness.  As \(s\to\infty\),
\[
J_m(s)=\frac{m}{s}+O(s^{-m-1})
\]
and \(\psi(s)=\log s+O(s^{-1})\).  Hence
\[
J_m(s)-\psi(s)\to-\infty.
\]
Since \(\Psi^{-1}(y)\to0\) as \(y\to-\infty\), we get
\[
R_m(s)=\Psi^{-1}\!\big(J_m(s)-\psi(s)\big)-s\to-\infty.
\]
Continuity then implies that \(R_m\) attains a finite maximum on \([1,\infty)\).  This proves the variational threshold criterion.

To see that the old endpoint criterion fails for \(m\ge4\), put
\[
A_m=\Psi^{-1}\!\big(J_m(1)-\psi(1)\big).
\]
Because \(J_m(1)=m/2\) and \(\psi(1)=-\gamma\), this is the point determined by
\[
\psi(A_m)=\frac m2+\gamma.
\]
Differentiating \(R_m(s)\) gives
\[
R_m'(s)=
\frac{J_m'(s)-\psi'(s)}
{\psi'\!\left(\Psi^{-1}(J_m(s)-\psi(s))\right)}
-1.
\]
At \(s=1\),
\[
J_m'(1)=\frac{m(m-2)}{4},
\]
and therefore
\[
R_m'(1)=
\frac{\frac{m(m-2)}4-\psi'(1)}{\psi'(A_m)}-1.
\]
For \(m\ge4\), the numerator \(\frac{m(m-2)}4-\psi'(1)\) is positive because \(\psi'(1)=\pi^2/6<2\).  Also \(A_m\ge A_4\), and \(\psi(A_4)=2+\gamma\).  Since \(\psi(x)<\log x\), we have \(A_4>e^{2+\gamma}>7\).  The integral comparison
\[
\psi'(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac1{(x+k)^2}<\int_{x-1}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^2}=\frac1{x-1}
\]
then gives \(\psi'(A_m)<1/6\).  Consequently
\[
R_m'(1)>6\left(2-\frac{\pi^2}{6}\right)-1>0.
\]
Thus \(s=1\) cannot be the maximizer for \(m\ge4\), and \(\rho_m>R_m(1)\).

Finally, if the maximum is attained at an interior point \(s_m>1\), then \(R_m(s_m)=\rho_m\) gives
\[
\psi(s_m+\rho_m)=J_m(s_m)-\psi(s_m),
\]
and the first-order condition \(R_m'(s_m)=0\) gives
\[
\psi'(s_m+\rho_m)=J_m'(s_m)-\psi'(s_m).
\]
This completes the reduction.

## Notes

- This theorem does not compute closed forms for \(\rho_m\).  It gives the exact variational threshold and proves that the left-endpoint formula from the monotone \(u'/u\) case fails for every \(m\ge4\).
- For \(m=1\) and \(m=2\), \(J_m\) is nonincreasing on \([1,\infty)\), so the endpoint theorem [[wiki/nodes/mrw-0fd149ddc79d|Sharp threshold for reciprocal Gamma-product monotonicity]] applies directly.
