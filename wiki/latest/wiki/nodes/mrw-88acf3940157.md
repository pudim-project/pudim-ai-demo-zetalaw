# Corollary: Endpoint interval triples force full terminal cross-shadow exclusions

## Statement

Let \(P=B\sqcup T\) be a finite disjoint decomposition, and let
\[
\mathcal F
=
\bigcup_{e\in\mathcal E}\{e\cup R:R\in\mathcal R_e\}
\subseteq2^P
\]
be pair-link-free.  For terminal families
\(\mathcal A,\mathcal B\subseteq2^T\), define the full cross-interval shadow
\[
\mathsf I_T(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)
=
\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal A,\ B\in\mathcal B}I_T(A,B).
\]
For a terminal family \(\mathcal A\), define the full two-point interval
shadow
\[
\mathsf J_T(\mathcal A)
=
\bigcup_{\substack{A,B\in\mathcal A\\A\ne B}}I_T(A,B).
\]

Then the following terminal exclusions hold.

1. If \(e_1,e_2,e_3\in\mathcal E\) are pairwise distinct endpoint patterns and
   \[
   e_3\in I_B(e_1,e_2),
   \]
   then
   \[
   \mathcal R_{e_3}
   \cap
   \mathsf I_T(\mathcal R_{e_1},\mathcal R_{e_2})
   =
   \varnothing.
   \]
   In particular,
   \[
   \mathcal R_{e_1}\cap\mathcal R_{e_2}\cap\mathcal R_{e_3}
   =
   \varnothing.
   \]

2. If \(e,f\in\mathcal E\) satisfy
   \[
   f\subsetneq e,
   \]
   then
   \[
   \mathcal R_f\cap\mathsf J_T(\mathcal R_e)=\varnothing.
   \]

The interval shadows in both conclusions are full interval shadows: terminal
endpoints are not removed.

## Proof

For the first claim, suppose
\[
R_3\in
\mathcal R_{e_3}
\cap
\mathsf I_T(\mathcal R_{e_1},\mathcal R_{e_2}).
\]
Then there are \(R_1\in\mathcal R_{e_1}\) and
\(R_2\in\mathcal R_{e_2}\) such that
\[
R_3\in I_T(R_1,R_2).
\]
Set
\[
S_i=e_i\cup R_i\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Because \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) are pairwise distinct, the ambient sets
\(S_1,S_2,S_3\) are pairwise distinct regardless of any coincidences among
\(R_1,R_2,R_3\).  By hypothesis,
\[
e_3\in I_B(e_1,e_2),
\qquad
R_3\in I_T(R_1,R_2).
\]
The interval factorization `mrw-20ca89f696f2` gives
\[
S_3\in I_P(S_1,S_2),
\]
contradicting pair-link-freeness of \(\mathcal F\).  Therefore the stated
cross-shadow intersection is empty.

If \(R\) lay in
\[
\mathcal R_{e_1}\cap\mathcal R_{e_2}\cap\mathcal R_{e_3},
\]
then \(R\in I_T(R,R)\).  Hence \(R\) would lie in the forbidden cross-shadow
from the first claim, proving the triple-intersection exclusion.

For the second claim, suppose
\[
R_3\in\mathcal R_f\cap\mathsf J_T(\mathcal R_e).
\]
Then there are distinct \(R_1,R_2\in\mathcal R_e\) such that
\[
R_3\in I_T(R_1,R_2).
\]
Since \(f\subsetneq e\), one has
\[
f\in I_B(e,e)=2^e.
\]
Define
\[
S_1=e\cup R_1,\qquad
S_2=e\cup R_2,\qquad
S_3=f\cup R_3.
\]
The sets \(S_1,S_2,S_3\) are pairwise distinct: \(S_1\ne S_2\) because
\(R_1\ne R_2\), and \(S_3\) has endpoint pattern \(f\ne e\).  Again
`mrw-20ca89f696f2` gives
\[
S_3\in I_P(S_1,S_2),
\]
contradicting pair-link-freeness.  Therefore
\[
\mathcal R_f\cap\mathsf J_T(\mathcal R_e)=\varnothing.
\]

## Notes

- This is a direct corollary of interval factorization, but it is the useful
  form for the escaped-mass branch: unshielded endpoint structure forces
  terminal fibers to avoid explicit full shadows.
- Full terminal shadows are essential.  In the first conclusion one may have
  \(R_1=R_2=R_3\), and the ambient sets are still distinct because endpoint
  patterns are distinct.  In the second conclusion \(R_3\) may equal one of
  the terminal interval endpoints, and the ambient middle set is still distinct
  because \(f\ne e\).
- The corollary does not prove terminal decay.  It identifies the exact
  terminal exclusions that must be charged by a future shadow-growth,
  cross-fiber overlap, or endpoint-support decomposition theorem.
