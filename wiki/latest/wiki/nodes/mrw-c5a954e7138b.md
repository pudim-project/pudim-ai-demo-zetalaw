# Problem: Finite-prime weighted fiber extremal problem for lcm triangles

## Statement

Let \(P\) be a finite set of primes and let \(\langle P\rangle\) be the multiplicative semigroup of \(P\)-smooth positive integers.  Define
\[
F_P(X)=
\max\left\{
|B\cap[1,X]|:\ B\subseteq\langle P\rangle,\ 
B\text{ contains no three distinct elements with equal pairwise lcm}
\right\}.
\]
The finite-prime weighted fiber problem is to prove useful bounds for
\[
\sum_{\substack{k\le N\\(k,M_P)=1}}F_P(N/k),
\qquad
M_P=\prod_{p\in P}p,
\]
strong enough to improve the known local packing upper bounds for Erdos #536.

The first concrete case is \(P=\{2,3\}\), where the problem becomes an extremal problem on the exponent grid \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2\) with forbidden corner triples
\[
(i+1,j),\qquad (i,j+1),\qquad (i+1,j+1).
\]
After the two-prime row-column theorem, the active next case is \(P=\{2,3,5\}\).

Equivalently, for finite \(P\), define
\[
\Gamma_P(X)=
\left\{\alpha\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^P:
\prod_{p\in P}p^{\alpha_p}\le X
\right\}
\]
and let \(g_P(X)\) be the largest size of a subset of \(\Gamma_P(X)\) with no three distinct vectors whose pairwise coordinatewise maxima are all equal.  The finite-prime weighted-grid reduction asks for bounds on
\[
\delta_P\int_1^\infty\frac{g_P(t)}{t^2}\,dt,
\qquad
\delta_P=\prod_{p\in P}\left(1-\frac1p\right).
\]
For \(P=\{2,3,5\}\), the immediate target is
\[
\frac4{15}\int_1^\infty\frac{g_{235}(t)}{t^2}\,dt<\frac56,
\]
or a proof that some natural route cannot achieve this.

## Evidence

- The obstruction [[wiki/nodes/mrw-efc6dd81fc95|Obstruction to naive finite-prime fiber lifting for lcm triangles]] shows that positive density alone need not create a rich single outside-kernel fiber.
- The packing proposition [[wiki/nodes/mrw-e844b4203305|Disjoint lcm-triangle packing upper bound for Erdos 536]] gives a first \(\{2,3\}\)-grid lower bound on the number of disjoint forbidden triples, yielding \(f(N)\le 11N/12+O((\log N)^2)\).
- The rectangular packing proposition [[wiki/nodes/mrw-c44269169b5b|Rectangular lcm-triangle packing improvements for Erdos 536]] improves the local disjoint-packing bound to
\[
f(N)\le \frac{42287}{46656}N+O(1).
\]
It also proves the dyadic multiscale bound
\[
f(N)\le
\left(1-\frac{\sigma_{2,3}}3\right)N+O((\log N)^2),
\qquad
\sigma_{2,3}=0.2807753443\ldots.
\]
It also proves the unit-corner model is saturated at exponent weight \(1/4\).
- The row-column fiber theorem [[wiki/nodes/mrw-41a967169307|Two-prime row-column fiber bound for Erdos 536]] proves the stronger overlapping-constraint bound
\[
f(N)\le N-\left\lfloor\frac N6\right\rfloor.
\]
- The finite-prime weighted-grid reduction [[wiki/nodes/mrw-f835f9671070|Finite-prime weighted-grid reduction for Erdos 536]] proves the exact bridge
\[
f(N)\le
\sum_{\substack{r\le N\\(r,Q_P)=1}}g_P(N/r),
\qquad
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{f(N)}N
\le
\delta_P\int_1^\infty\frac{g_P(t)}{t^2}\,dt.
\]
- The pair-slice obstruction [[wiki/nodes/mrw-34f73025a206|Pair-slice obstruction for three-prime Erdos 536 weighted grids]] proves that applying the two-prime row-column theorem inside fixed \(5\)-adic slices of the \(P=\{2,3,5\}\) grid gives exactly the old \(5/6\) constant.  Hence a three-prime improvement must use cross-slice balanced triangles.
- The finite-prefix certificate [[wiki/nodes/mrw-a261a0a4df25|Finite box certificate for the three-prime Erdos 536 weighted grid]] proves the corrected three-prime integral bound
\[
\int_1^\infty g_{235}(t)t^{-2}\,dt\le\frac{149}{48},
\]
and therefore improves the local #536 density constant to
\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{f(N)}N\le\frac{149}{180}<\frac56.
\]
It also records a transfer obstruction: the raw weighted independent-set certificate \(743/300\) on \([0,2]^3\) is not itself the finite-prime prefix-rank integral.
- The extended prefix-rank certificate [[wiki/nodes/mrw-3367b245c458|Extended prefix-rank certificate for the three-prime Erdos 536 grid]] improves this by exact finite prefix ranks through threshold \(162\):
\[
\int_1^\infty g_{235}(t)t^{-2}\,dt\le\frac{197623}{64800},
\]
and therefore
\[
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{f(N)}N
\le
\frac{197623}{243000}
=0.8132633744\ldots.
\]
This remains constant-density progress only.  The next target is no longer another bare finite table; it is to extract a reusable rank-cover, weighted-cover, or scaling obstruction from these finite-prefix savings.
- The fixed finite-prime axis-floor obstruction [[wiki/nodes/mrw-2060f97aad60|Fixed finite-prime axis-floor obstruction for Erdos 536]] proves that every fixed finite \(P\) has
\[
\delta_P\int_1^\infty g_P(t)t^{-2}\,dt
\ge
\delta_P\left(1+\sum_{p\in P}\frac1{p-1}\right).
\]
For \(P=\{2,3,5\}\), this lower floor is \(11/15\).  Thus fixed \(P=\{2,3,5\}\) can still improve constants but cannot be the terminal \(f(N)=o(N)\) route.
- The low-support growing-prime criterion [[wiki/nodes/mrw-4daa694d9526|Low-support growing-prime criterion for Erdos 536]] proves a concrete terminal bridge.  For \(0\le\theta<1\), let \(S_P=\sum_{p\in P}1/p\), let \(L_{P,\theta}(t)\) count vectors in \(\Gamma_P(t)\) with support size at most \(\theta S_P\), and put
\[
R_P(\theta)=
\delta_P\int_1^\infty
\bigl(g_P(t)-L_{P,\theta}(t)\bigr)_+t^{-2}\,dt.
\]
If \(S_{P_j}\to\infty\) and \(R_{P_j}(\theta)\to0\) for some growing prime sets \(P_j\), then \(f(N)=o(N)\).  Thus the next obstruction is the high-support residual \(R_P(\theta)\), not another fixed-\(P\) prefix table.
- The squarefree binary-choice obstruction [[wiki/nodes/mrw-9afb17b1b84a|Binary-choice squarefree obstruction to pointwise support envelopes]] proves that pointwise support-only envelopes are false: for the first \(2m\) primes grouped into pairs, there are \(2^m\) squarefree grid-bad-free vectors all of support \(m\), while the low-support count at the containing prefix is only \(\exp(O_\theta((\log m)^2))\).  The same note proves the obstruction's harmonic mass is at most \(1/m!\), and more generally every block-transversal spike of support \(r\) has harmonic mass at most \(e^{-r}\).  Therefore this is not a genuine obstruction to \(R_P(\theta)\to0\); it quarantines pointwise/cardinality-only support arguments and makes biased-measure control the next target.
- Oracle's advisory audit identified this weighted fiber problem as the next route; it is accepted only as an open local target, not as a proved theorem.

## Notes

- This problem is the replacement for the false single-fiber lifting heuristic.
- The immediate unit-corner packing target is closed: unit corners cannot beat exponent weight \(1/4\).
- The finite rectangular disjoint-packing target improves the constant but remains far from \(f(N)=o(N)\).
- The two-prime overlapping row-column mechanism supersedes disjoint packing as the active local method.
- The general finite-prime integral criterion is now proved.  The next target is no longer pair-slicing; it is a genuinely three-prime weighted-cover, projection-energy, or finite certificate mechanism that exploits balanced non-corner triples.
- The first genuine three-prime finite-prefix certificate improves \(5/6\) by \(1/180\) in density.  The next route should extend prefix-rank savings or replace the pair-slice tail by a stronger weighted cover; the constant bound is progress only, not a solution of \(f(N)=o(N)\).
- The extended finite-prefix certificate improves the local density constant further to \(197623/243000\).  This proves the finite-prefix mechanism is live, but it also increases the risk of nonterminal computation-chasing; further progress should compress the finite data into a general rank-cover or weighted-cover principle.
- The fixed-prime axis floor changes the next bridge: a terminal finite-prime strategy should now target a growing-\(P\) theorem with constants tending to \(0\), or a non-fixed-fiber mechanism.  Further fixed-\(P\) tables are secondary unless they expose such a scalable mechanism.
- The low-support criterion names the next progress invariant \(R_P(\theta)\).  Proving its decay for a concrete family such as the first \(k\) primes would settle #536; constructing nonvanishing high-support residual mass would rigorously obstruct this support-tail route.
- The squarefree binary-choice diagnostic shows that high support alone does not force a grid-bad triple, even with exponentially many high-support squarefree vectors.  The next route must prove or refute a biased harmonic product-measure statement, not a pointwise high-support cardinality statement.
