# Theorem: Support localization for polynomial Gamma-threshold maximizers

## Statement

Let \(m\ge4\), and keep the notation of [[wiki/nodes/mrw-37311e7a5a0f|Variational sharp threshold for polynomial Gamma-product monotonicity]]:
\[
J_m(s)=\frac{m s^{m-1}}{s^m+1},
\qquad
R_m(s)=\psi^{-1}\!\big(J_m(s)-\psi(s)\big)-s,
\qquad s\ge1.
\]
If \(s_m\) is any point at which \(R_m\) attains its global maximum on \([1,\infty)\), then
\[
1<s_m<(m-1)^{1/m}.
\]
More sharply, every maximizer satisfies
\[
J_m'(s_m)>\psi'(s_m).
\]
Consequently, as \(m\to\infty\), every maximizer satisfies the uniform localization
\[
s_m=1+O\!\left(\frac{\log m}{m}\right).
\]

## Proof

By [[wiki/nodes/mrw-37311e7a5a0f|the variational threshold theorem]], \(R_m\) is continuous, tends to \(-\infty\), and attains its maximum.  The same theorem proves \(R_m'(1)>0\) for \(m\ge4\), so no global maximizer can occur at the left endpoint \(s=1\).

Write
\[
A_m(s)=\psi^{-1}\!\big(J_m(s)-\psi(s)\big).
\]
Since \(\psi'(x)>0\) for \(x>0\), differentiation gives
\[
R_m'(s)=\frac{J_m'(s)-\psi'(s)}{\psi'(A_m(s))}-1.
\]
The derivative of \(J_m\) is
\[
J_m'(s)
=\frac{m s^{m-2}\big((m-1)-s^m\big)}{(s^m+1)^2}.
\]
Thus \(J_m'(s)\le0\) for \(s\ge (m-1)^{1/m}\).  For such \(s\),
\[
J_m'(s)-\psi'(s)<0,
\]
and therefore \(R_m'(s)<0\).  Hence \(R_m\) is strictly decreasing on
\[
\big[(m-1)^{1/m},\infty\big].
\]
Because \(R_m'(1)>0\), the maximum is not at \(1\), and because \(R_m\) is already decreasing at and to the right of \((m-1)^{1/m}\), no maximizer can lie there.  This proves
\[
1<s_m<(m-1)^{1/m}.
\]

At an interior maximizer, \(R_m'(s_m)=0\).  Therefore
\[
J_m'(s_m)-\psi'(s_m)=\psi'(A_m(s_m))>0,
\]
which gives the sharper support condition \(J_m'(s_m)>\psi'(s_m)\).

Finally,
\[
(m-1)^{1/m}=\exp\left(\frac{\log(m-1)}{m}\right).
\]
For \(m\ge4\), \(0<\log(m-1)/m<1\), and the elementary inequality \(e^x-1\le2x\) on \(0\le x\le1\) gives
\[
0<s_m-1<(m-1)^{1/m}-1
\le \frac{2\log(m-1)}{m}
\le \frac{2\log m}{m}.
\]
This proves the asserted large-\(m\) localization.

## Notes

- This is a genuine local theorem but only a partial answer to the polynomial threshold problem.  It does not prove uniqueness of the maximizer or compute the exact threshold \(\rho_m\).
- The result is still useful because it moves every \(m\ge4\) maximizer into the short interval \(1<s<(m-1)^{1/m}\), whose length is \(O(\log m/m)\).
