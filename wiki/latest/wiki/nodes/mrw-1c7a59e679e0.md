# Corollary: Complete product towers have a Poisson endpoint-factor envelope

## Statement

Consider a fixed finite product of complete multipartite transversal endpoint
levels.  Let the individual endpoint parts be indexed by a fixed finite
nonempty set \(\mathcal P\), and put
\[
K=|\mathcal P|.
\]
For each part \(P\in\mathcal P\), suppose the endpoint weights are diffuse:
\[
\delta_P=\max_{x\in P}q_x\to0,
\qquad
\sum_{x\in P}q_x\to \beta_P<\infty.
\]
Let
\[
A=\sum_{P\in\mathcal P}\beta_P.
\]
Then the endpoint factor \(\Gamma_n\) of the complete product tower tends to
\[
\Gamma_\infty
=
e^{-A}\prod_{P\in\mathcal P}\beta_P.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\Gamma_\infty
\le
e^{-A}\left(\frac{A}{K}\right)^K
\le
e^{-K},
\]
with the convention that \(\Gamma_\infty=0\) if some \(\beta_P=0\).  Equality
throughout the full chain occurs exactly when \(\beta_P=1\) for every
\(P\in\mathcal P\).

Thus the exact complete product-tower residual has the asymptotic
endpoint-factor envelope
\[
\mathcal R_n(L)
=
\Gamma_n\mathfrak M_T(L-K)
\le
\left(e^{-A}\left(\frac{A}{K}\right)^K+o(1)\right)\mathfrak M_T(L-K)
\le
\left(e^{-K}+o(1)\right)\mathfrak M_T(L-K).
\]
This is not an exact finite-\(n\) inequality and not a terminal contraction:
the terminal factor \(\mathfrak M_T(L-K)\) remains uncontrolled.

## Proof

By `mrw-cd7b1fe1d9af`, the complete product tower has exact residual
\[
\Gamma\,\mathfrak M_T(L-K),
\]
where \(\Gamma\) is the product, over all endpoint parts \(P\in\mathcal P\),
of the probability \(a_P\) of selecting exactly one point from \(P\).  For a
single part \(P\),
\[
a_P
=
\sum_{x\in P}q_x\prod_{u\in P\setminus\{x\}}(1-q_u)
=
\left(\prod_{u\in P}(1-q_u)\right)
\sum_{x\in P}\frac{q_x}{1-q_x}.
\]
The diffuse assumptions imply
\[
\sum_{x\in P}q_x^2\le \delta_P\sum_{x\in P}q_x\to0.
\]
Therefore
\[
\log\prod_{u\in P}(1-q_u)
=
-\sum_{u\in P}q_u+O\!\left(\sum_{u\in P}q_u^2\right)
\to -\beta_P,
\]
and
\[
\sum_{x\in P}\frac{q_x}{1-q_x}
=
\sum_{x\in P}q_x+O\!\left(\sum_{x\in P}q_x^2\right)
\to \beta_P.
\]
Hence
\[
a_P\to \beta_Pe^{-\beta_P}.
\]
Multiplying over the fixed finite set of parts gives
\[
\Gamma_\infty
=
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P}\beta_Pe^{-\beta_P}
=
e^{-A}\prod_{P\in\mathcal P}\beta_P.
\]

For fixed \(A\), the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives
\[
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P}\beta_P
\le
\left(\frac{A}{K}\right)^K,
\]
with equality when all \(\beta_P=A/K\), provided all \(\beta_P\) are positive.
If some \(\beta_P=0\), then \(\Gamma_\infty=0\), and the displayed inequality
still holds.  Finally, the function
\[
f(A)=e^{-A}\left(\frac{A}{K}\right)^K
\]
has
\[
\frac{d}{dA}\log f(A)=-1+\frac{K}{A},
\]
so its maximum on \(A>0\) occurs at \(A=K\), where \(f(K)=e^{-K}\).  Therefore
\[
\Gamma_n
\to
\Gamma_\infty
\le
e^{-A}\left(\frac{A}{K}\right)^K
\le
e^{-K}.
\]
Multiplying the asymptotic endpoint-coefficient bound by the exact shifted
terminal residual \(\mathfrak M_T(L-K)\) gives the displayed residual
envelope with \(o(1)\) in the endpoint coefficient.

## Notes

- The result applies to fixed finite products of complete multipartite
  transversal levels with \(K\ge1\).  It does not address growing \(K\),
  growing tower depth, or non-complete/non-transversal shielded families
  without further work.
- The bound \(e^{-K}\) is sharp for the endpoint factor alone: take all part
  intensities equal to \(1\), so \(A=K\).
- This is an asymptotic endpoint-factor contraction, but not a full
  shifted-window contraction.  A terminal theorem still needs control of
  \(\mathfrak M_T(L-K)\).
