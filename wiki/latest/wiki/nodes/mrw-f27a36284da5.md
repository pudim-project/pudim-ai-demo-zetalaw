# Corollary: Dyadic lower obstruction for the n=2 polygamma beta window

## Statement

Let
\[
C_2(x)=\psi''(x)+x\psi^{(3)}(x),
\qquad
P_2(x)=\psi''(x)\psi''(1/x),
\]
and define
\[
\lambda_{2,1/2}
=
\frac{
\log\!\left(
\frac{56\zeta(3)(\zeta(3)-1)}{\pi^4-28\zeta(3)}
\right)}
{\log(1/2)}.
\]
If
\[
x^\beta C_2(x)-P_2(x)<0\qquad(x>0),
\]
then
\[
\beta>\lambda_{2,1/2}.
\]
Moreover
\[
\lambda_{2,1/2}>2,
\qquad
\lambda_{2,1/2}\approx2.2286936706.
\]
Combining this pointwise obstruction with [[wiki/nodes/mrw-8a146667d25b|Endpoint exclusion for the n=2 polygamma beta window]], every admissible \(n=2\) parameter must lie in
\[
(\lambda_{2,1/2},3].
\]

## Proof

By [[wiki/nodes/mrw-0241ab931d33|Even-order envelope reduction for polygamma beta windows]], admissibility in the \(n=2\) even-order window implies
\[
\beta>Q_2(x)
\qquad(0<x<1),
\]
where
\[
Q_2(x)=\frac{\log(P_2(x)/C_2(x))}{\log x}.
\]
It is therefore enough to evaluate the single point \(x=1/2\).

The standard special values are
\[
\psi''(1/2)=-14\zeta(3),
\qquad
\psi^{(3)}(1/2)=\pi^4,
\]
and the recurrence for \(\psi''\) gives
\[
\psi''(2)=2-2\zeta(3).
\]
Thus
\[
C_2(1/2)=\frac{\pi^4}{2}-14\zeta(3),
\]
and
\[
P_2(1/2)=(-14\zeta(3))(2-2\zeta(3))
=28\zeta(3)(\zeta(3)-1).
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac{P_2(1/2)}{C_2(1/2)}
=
\frac{56\zeta(3)(\zeta(3)-1)}{\pi^4-28\zeta(3)}.
\]
The pointwise envelope condition at \(x=1/2\) gives
\[
\beta>
\frac{\log(P_2(1/2)/C_2(1/2))}{\log(1/2)}
=\lambda_{2,1/2}.
\]

It remains to prove that this obstruction is genuinely stronger than \(\beta>2\).  Since \(0<1/2<1\),
\[
\lambda_{2,1/2}>2
\]
is equivalent to
\[
\frac{56\zeta(3)(\zeta(3)-1)}{\pi^4-28\zeta(3)}<\frac14.
\]
Writing \(z=\zeta(3)\), this is equivalent to
\[
224z^2-196z<\pi^4.
\]
The elementary bounds
\[
1<z<1+\frac18+\frac1{27}+\frac1{64}+\int_4^\infty t^{-3}\,dt<\frac{121}{100}
\]
and \(\pi>31/10\) give
\[
224z^2-196z
<
224\left(\frac{121}{100}\right)^2
-196\left(\frac{121}{100}\right)
<91
<
\left(\frac{31}{10}\right)^4
<\pi^4.
\]
Here \(224t^2-196t\) is increasing for \(t>7/16\), and \(z>1\).  This proves the strict inequality and hence \(\lambda_{2,1/2}>2\).  The displayed decimal value is only a numerical orientation for the exact constant.

## Notes

- This is a pointwise dyadic obstruction, not the exact lower endpoint for the \(n=2\) scalar-envelope problem.
- The right endpoint \(\beta=3\) for \(n=2\) is proved admissible in [[wiki/nodes/mrw-201bbda2c917|Right endpoint theorem for even-order polygamma beta windows]].
- The stronger certified pointwise obstruction \(\beta>231/100\) is proved in [[wiki/nodes/mrw-30f9a055fa9a|Certified two-sevenths lower obstruction for the n=2 beta window]].
- The remaining \(n=2\) target is to certify the global scalar envelope
\[
\sup_{0<x<1}Q_2(x)
\]
not to collect unaudited numerical samples.
