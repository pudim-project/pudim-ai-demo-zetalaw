# Corollary: Prime-biased endpoint atoms quarantine diagonal high-window energy

## Statement

Let \(B\) be finite and let \(\pi_B\) be the product law on \(2^B\),
\[
\pi_B(e)=\prod_{b\in e}q_b\prod_{b\notin e}(1-q_b),
\qquad
0<q_b\le\frac12.
\]
Set
\[
P_0(B)=\prod_{b\in B}(1-q_b),
\qquad
Q(B)=\sum_{b\in B}q_b.
\]
Let \(\varnothing\ne\mathcal E\subseteq2^B\), let
\[
\Lambda=\pi_B(\mathcal E)>0,
\qquad
\lambda_e=\pi_B(e)\quad(e\in\mathcal E),
\qquad
w_e=\lambda_e/\Lambda.
\]
Then
\[
\eta:=\max_{e\in\mathcal E}w_e
\le
\frac{P_0(B)}{\Lambda}
\le
\frac{e^{-Q(B)}}{\Lambda},
\]
and
\[
H_2(w):=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}w_e^2
\le
\frac{P_0(B)}{\Lambda}
\le
\frac{e^{-Q(B)}}{\Lambda}.
\]

In any endpoint-fiber instance to which `mrw-724d68db9b8c` applies, with
\(\tau>0\), \(M>0\), and
\[
\rho=\frac{M}{\Lambda\tau}>0,
\]
one has
\[
\frac{\Delta}{M^2/\tau}
\le
\frac{P_0(B)}{\Lambda\rho}
\le
\frac{e^{-Q(B)}}{\Lambda\rho}.
\]

Moreover, in the combined branch setting of `mrw-ad1f6f41665a` and
`mrw-90be6f9a7f88`, if
\[
\Omega_{\mathcal C}\le\gamma\frac{M^2}{\tau}
\]
and
\[
\gamma+\frac{P_0(B)}{\Lambda\rho}<1,
\]
then
\[
\Omega_{\mathcal N}
\ge
\left(1-\gamma-\frac{P_0(B)}{\Lambda\rho}\right)
\frac{M^2}{\tau}.
\]
Consequently, there exists a distinct positive-weight nonchargeable endpoint
pair \((e,f)\) such that
\[
\mathcal E\cap I_B(e,f)\subseteq\{e,f\}
\]
and
\[
\frac{\nu_T(\mathcal R_e\cap\mathcal R_f\cap H)}{\tau}
\ge
\left(1-\gamma-\frac{P_0(B)}{\Lambda\rho}\right)\rho^2.
\]

Conversely, if for some \(\delta>0\),
\[
\Delta\ge\delta\frac{M^2}{\tau},
\]
then
\[
\Lambda
\le
\frac{P_0(B)}{\delta\rho}
\le
\frac{e^{-Q(B)}}{\delta\rho}.
\]
Thus diagonal-heavy energy at fixed positive \(\rho\) is possible only when
the endpoint support mass \(\Lambda\) is at most the empty-atom scale up to the
factor \(1/(\delta\rho)\).

## Proof

For every \(e\subseteq B\),
\[
\pi_B(e)
=
\prod_{b\in e}q_b\prod_{b\notin e}(1-q_b)
=
P_0(B)\prod_{b\in e}\frac{q_b}{1-q_b}.
\]
Since \(q_b\le1/2\), each factor satisfies
\[
\frac{q_b}{1-q_b}\le1.
\]
Therefore
\[
\pi_B(e)\le P_0(B)
\]
for all \(e\subseteq B\).  Hence
\[
\eta
=
\max_{e\in\mathcal E}\frac{\pi_B(e)}{\Lambda}
\le
\frac{P_0(B)}{\Lambda}.
\]

For the Herfindahl bound,
\[
H_2(w)
=
\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\left(\frac{\pi_B(e)}{\Lambda}\right)^2
=
\frac{\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\pi_B(e)^2}{\Lambda^2}.
\]
Using \(\pi_B(e)\le P_0(B)\),
\[
\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\pi_B(e)^2
\le
P_0(B)\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\pi_B(e)
=
P_0(B)\Lambda.
\]
Thus
\[
H_2(w)\le\frac{P_0(B)}{\Lambda}.
\]

Also,
\[
\log P_0(B)
=
\sum_{b\in B}\log(1-q_b)
\le
-\sum_{b\in B}q_b
=
-Q(B),
\]
so
\[
P_0(B)\le e^{-Q(B)}.
\]

Now apply the max-atom diagonal estimate from `mrw-724d68db9b8c`:
\[
\frac{\Delta}{M^2/\tau}\le\frac{\eta}{\rho}.
\]
Since \(\eta\le P_0(B)/\Lambda\),
\[
\frac{\Delta}{M^2/\tau}
\le
\frac{P_0(B)}{\Lambda\rho}
\le
\frac{e^{-Q(B)}}{\Lambda\rho}.
\]

For the branch consequence, set
\[
\kappa=\frac{P_0(B)}{\Lambda\rho}.
\]
The preceding estimate gives
\[
\Delta\le\kappa\frac{M^2}{\tau}.
\]
Together with
\[
\Omega_{\mathcal C}\le\gamma\frac{M^2}{\tau},
\]
the total-energy lower bound from `mrw-ad1f6f41665a` and the split from
`mrw-90be6f9a7f88` give
\[
\Omega_{\mathcal N}
\ge
(1-\gamma-\kappa)\frac{M^2}{\tau}.
\]
Substituting \(\kappa=P_0(B)/(\Lambda\rho)\) proves the displayed lower bound.

Let
\[
\alpha=1-\gamma-\frac{P_0(B)}{\Lambda\rho}>0.
\]
Then
\[
\Omega_{\mathcal N}\ge\alpha\frac{M^2}{\tau}
=
\alpha\rho^2\Lambda^2\tau.
\]
Since the total endpoint-pair weight in the distinct nonchargeable sum is at
most \(\Lambda^2\), some distinct nonchargeable pair \((e,f)\) with
\(\pi_B(e)\pi_B(f)>0\) satisfies
\[
\nu_T(\mathcal R_e\cap\mathcal R_f\cap H)
\ge
\alpha\rho^2\tau.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\frac{\nu_T(\mathcal R_e\cap\mathcal R_f\cap H)}{\tau}
\ge
\alpha\rho^2.
\]
By nonchargeability,
\[
\mathcal E\cap I_B(e,f)\subseteq\{e,f\}.
\]

Finally, if
\[
\Delta\ge\delta\frac{M^2}{\tau},
\]
then the diagonal upper bound implies
\[
\delta
\le
\frac{\Delta}{M^2/\tau}
\le
\frac{P_0(B)}{\Lambda\rho}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\Lambda\le\frac{P_0(B)}{\delta\rho}
\le
\frac{e^{-Q(B)}}{\delta\rho}.
\]

## Used by

- Future concentration quarantine in prime-coordinate endpoint models:
  diagonal-heavy energy at positive \(\rho\) forces small endpoint support mass
  \(\Lambda\) at the empty-atom scale.
- Future escaped-mass arguments: when \(\Lambda\rho\gg P_0(B)\) and
  chargeable energy is small, the distinct nonchargeable branch must carry
  positive high-window common overlap.
- Future endpoint-depth arguments: if \(Q(B)\) is large while \(\Lambda\) and
  \(\rho\) are not exponentially small, diagonal energy is negligible.

## Notes

- This is a quarantine corollary, not terminal Erdos 536 evidence.  It does not
  prove that \(\Lambda\) or \(\rho\) is bounded below in the active model.
- The bound \(P_0(B)/\Lambda\) can exceed \(1\), so the estimate is useful only
  when the endpoint support mass is large relative to the empty atom.  The
  trivial bounds \(\eta,H_2(w)\le1\) remain available.
- The assumption \(q_b\le1/2\) is exactly what makes the empty atom a maximum
  atom.  Without it, \(P_0(B)\) should be replaced by
  \(\prod_b\max\{q_b,1-q_b\}\).
- No new pair-link-free hypothesis is used for the accounting/local-shield
  conclusion.  Pair-link-freeness is needed only in later steps that convert
  endpoint shielding into terminal exclusions.
