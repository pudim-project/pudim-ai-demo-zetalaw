# Corollary: Relative high-window mass splits into endpoint support and fiber density

## Statement

Let \(B\) carry an endpoint product law \(\pi_B\), let
\(\mathcal E\subseteq2^B\), and let \(T,\nu_T,H\) be as in
`mrw-0845a9abe5b6`.  Assume
\[
\tau=\nu_T(H)>0.
\]
For terminal fibers \(\mathcal R_e\subseteq2^T\), define
\[
M=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\pi_B(e)\nu_T(\mathcal R_e\cap H),
\qquad
\Lambda=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\pi_B(e).
\]
Define
\[
\rho=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{M}{\Lambda\tau},&\Lambda>0,\\[1ex]
0,&\Lambda=0.
\end{cases}
\]

If \(\Lambda>0\), then
\[
\frac{M}{\tau}=\Lambda\rho,
\]
and
\[
0\le\rho\le1.
\]

For any \(\lambda,r>0\), if
\[
\Lambda\ge\lambda
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\rho\ge r,
\]
then
\[
M\ge\lambda r\,\tau.
\]
Equivalently, if
\[
M<\lambda r\,\tau,
\]
then
\[
\Lambda<\lambda
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\rho<r.
\]

Now assume the endpoint-intensity hypotheses of `mrw-4a7cdb250fd4`, with
fixed trichotomy parameter
\[
0<\varepsilon<1-\gamma.
\]
If the empty-atom branch holds and
\[
Q(B)>\log\frac1{\varepsilon\lambda r},
\]
then
\[
\Lambda<\lambda
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\rho<r.
\]
Equivalently, under
\[
\Lambda\ge\lambda,
\qquad
\rho\ge r,
\qquad
Q(B)>\log\frac1{\varepsilon\lambda r},
\]
the empty-atom branch is impossible.  Hence, assuming the trichotomy from
`mrw-0845a9abe5b6`, at least one of the chargeable-energy branch or the
distinct nonchargeable shield branch holds.

For a sequence with fixed \(\varepsilon,\lambda,r>0\) and
\[
Q(B_n)\to\infty,
\]
every empty-atom-branch subsequence eventually satisfies
\[
\Lambda_n<\lambda
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\rho_n<r.
\]

## Proof

If \(\Lambda>0\), the definition gives
\[
\rho=\frac{M}{\Lambda\tau},
\]
hence
\[
\frac{M}{\tau}=\Lambda\rho.
\]
Also
\[
0\le\nu_T(\mathcal R_e\cap H)\le\nu_T(H)=\tau,
\]
so
\[
0\le M\le\Lambda\tau.
\]
Therefore
\[
0\le\rho\le1.
\]

Let \(\lambda,r>0\).  If
\[
\Lambda\ge\lambda
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\rho\ge r,
\]
then necessarily \(\Lambda>0\), and
\[
\frac{M}{\tau}
=
\Lambda\rho
\ge
\lambda r.
\]
Thus
\[
M\ge\lambda r\,\tau.
\]
The contrapositive gives
\[
M<\lambda r\,\tau
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Lambda<\lambda\text{ or }\rho<r.
\]

For the endpoint-intensity claim, `mrw-4a7cdb250fd4` gives, on the
empty-atom branch,
\[
\frac{M}{\tau}\le\frac{e^{-Q(B)}}{\varepsilon}.
\]
If
\[
Q(B)>\log\frac1{\varepsilon\lambda r},
\]
then
\[
\frac{e^{-Q(B)}}{\varepsilon}<\lambda r.
\]
Therefore
\[
\frac{M}{\tau}<\lambda r,
\]
so the support-density split gives
\[
\Lambda<\lambda
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\rho<r.
\]
Consequently, if instead
\[
\Lambda\ge\lambda
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\rho\ge r,
\]
the empty-atom branch cannot hold.  The trichotomy of `mrw-0845a9abe5b6`
then leaves at least one of the remaining two branches.

Finally, if \(Q(B_n)\to\infty\), then for fixed
\(\varepsilon,\lambda,r>0\),
\[
Q(B_n)>\log\frac1{\varepsilon\lambda r}
\]
eventually.  Applying the preceding conclusion termwise proves the sequence
statement.

## Depends on

- `mrw-4a7cdb250fd4`: endpoint intensity quarantines the empty-atom branch.
- `mrw-0845a9abe5b6`: empty-atom mass or chargeable-or-shield trichotomy.

## Used by

- Future branch control: the missing positive relative mass lower bound
  \(M/\tau\ge m\) is exactly the product of endpoint support mass \(\Lambda\)
  and relative fiber high-window density \(\rho\).
- Future residual classification: in growing endpoint-intensity blocks, an
  empty-atom branch must eventually be endpoint-support-light or
  fiber-density-sparse for any fixed \(\lambda,r>0\).

## Notes

- This is an algebraic routing lemma, not terminal Erdos 536 evidence.
- It does not prove a lower bound on \(M/\tau\).  It decomposes that missing
  lower bound into two future tasks: endpoint support mass and relative
  high-window fiber density.
- The density \(\rho\) is the endpoint-support-weighted average
  \[
  \rho=
  \sum_{e\in\mathcal E}
  \frac{\pi_B(e)}{\Lambda}
  \frac{\nu_T(\mathcal R_e\cap H)}{\tau}
  \qquad(\Lambda>0).
  \]
- Thresholds \(r>1\) make \(\rho\ge r\) impossible; this is harmless, since
  the useful regime has \(0<r\le1\).
