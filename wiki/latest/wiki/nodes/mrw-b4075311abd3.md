# Proposition: Union-free reformulation of the biased lower-shadow route

## Statement

Let \(P\) be a finite set and let \(\mathcal F\subseteq2^P\).  The following
conditions are equivalent.

1. \(\mathcal F\) is lower-shadow union-cover-free: there are no
   \(A,B,C\in\mathcal F\) such that
   \[
   A,B\subsetneq C,\qquad A\ne B,\qquad A\cup B=C.
   \]
2. \(\mathcal F\) is union-free in the usual set-family sense: there are no
   three distinct \(A,B,C\in\mathcal F\) such that
   \[
   A\cup B=C.
   \]

Consequently, the biased lower-shadow union-cover problem
[[mrw-d0402aea6f58]] is exactly the prime-biased high-support union-free
problem under the product law \(\nu_P(p\in S)=1/p\).  In particular, if for
\(P_k=\{p_1,\ldots,p_k\}\), every fixed \(0\le\theta<1\), and every
\(\eta>0\), every union-free
\[
\mathcal F\subseteq H_{P_k,\theta}
\]
has \(\nu_{P_k}(\mathcal F)<\eta\) for all sufficiently large \(k\), then
\[
M_{P_k}(\theta)\to0.
\]

## Proof

If the lower-shadow condition fails, then \(A,B,C\) are three distinct family
members because \(A,B\subsetneq C\) and \(A\ne B\), and \(A\cup B=C\).  Thus
the usual union-free condition fails.

Conversely, suppose \(A,B,C\in\mathcal F\) are three distinct members with
\(A\cup B=C\).  Then \(A\subseteq C\) and \(B\subseteq C\).  Since \(A\ne C\)
and \(B\ne C\), both inclusions are proper.  Hence the lower-shadow
union-cover condition fails.

The reformulation of [[mrw-d0402aea6f58]] follows by substituting this
equivalence into its statement.  Finally, by
[[mrw-3c39ca3d1973]], any lower-shadow union cover is a squarefree
cosunflower triple.  Therefore a vanishing upper bound for positive-mass
high-support union-free families forces the squarefree residual
\(M_{P_k}(\theta)\) to vanish.

## Notes

- This is a nomenclature correction and exact reformulation, not a new
  solution of the squarefree residual route.
- Partition-free families are adjacent but not identical: they require the two
  lower sets to be disjoint, while union-free families forbid arbitrary
  \(A\cup B=C\) among three distinct members.
