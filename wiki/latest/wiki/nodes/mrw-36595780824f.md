# Proposition: Near-complete lower corridors concentrate on one ancestor signature pair

## Statement

Let \(P\) be a finite set and let \(\mathcal F\subseteq2^P\) be pair-link-free.
For \(R\subseteq P\), write
\[
G_R^\mathcal F=(P\setminus R,E_R^\mathcal F),
\qquad
\{x,y\}\in E_R^\mathcal F
\Longleftrightarrow
R\cup\{x,y\}\in\mathcal F .
\]

Let \(m\ge1\), and let \(Q\subseteq R_i\subseteq P\) for \(1\le i\le m\).
Let \(V\subseteq P\setminus\bigcup_iR_i\), and suppose that for each \(i\) the
upper slice graph \(G_{R_i}^\mathcal F\) contains a nondegenerate complete
bipartite graph
\[
K_{X_i,Y_i}
\qquad\text{on}\qquad
V=X_i\sqcup Y_i .
\]
For \(v\in V\), define the ancestor signature
\[
\sigma(v)=(\mathbf 1_{v\in X_i})_{i=1}^m\in\{0,1\}^m,
\]
and put \(V_\tau=\{v\in V:\sigma(v)=\tau\}\) and
\(\bar\tau=\mathbf 1-\tau\).

Let \(U,W\subseteq V\) be nonempty disjoint sets.  Give \(U\) and \(W\)
nonnegative weights \(a_u\) and \(b_w\), and assume positive side masses
\[
A=\sum_{u\in U}a_u>0,
\qquad
B=\sum_{w\in W}b_w>0 .
\]
For \(\tau\in\{0,1\}^m\), define side signature weights
\[
A_\tau=\sum_{u\in U\cap V_\tau}a_u,
\qquad
B_\tau=\sum_{w\in W\cap V_\tau}b_w .
\]
Let the lower edge mass across \(U|W\) be
\[
M_Q(U,W)=
\sum_{\substack{u\in U,\ w\in W\\ \{u,w\}\in E_Q^\mathcal F}} a_ub_w .
\]
Then
\[
M_Q(U,W)
\le
\sum_{\{\tau,\bar\tau\}}(A_\tau B_{\bar\tau}+A_{\bar\tau}B_\tau),
\tag{1}
\]
where the sum is over unordered complementary signature pairs.
Equivalently,
\[
M_Q(U,W)\le\sum_{\tau\in\{0,1\}^m}A_\tau B_{\bar\tau}.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently, if \(1/2\le\lambda\le1\) and
\[
M_Q(U,W)\ge\lambda AB,
\tag{3}
\]
then there is an ancestor signature \(\tau^*\in\{0,1\}^m\) such that
\[
A_{\tau^*}\ge\lambda A,
\qquad
B_{\bar\tau^*}\ge\lambda B.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus a near-complete lower corridor across \(U|W\) is near-pure with respect
to all ancestor signatures: outside one complementary ancestor-signature pair,
at most a \(1-\lambda\) fraction of each side weight remains.

In the equality case \(M_Q(U,W)=AB\), all positive \(U\)-weight lies in
\(V_{\tau^*}\), all positive \(W\)-weight lies in \(V_{\bar\tau^*}\), and every
positive-weight pair across these two supports is an edge of \(G_Q^\mathcal F\).

## Proof

By [[mrw-fced7420b905]], every lower edge
\[
\{x,y\}\in E_Q^\mathcal F\cap\binom V2
\]
joins complementary ancestor signatures:
\[
\sigma(x)=\mathbf 1-\sigma(y).
\tag{5}
\]
Therefore every edge counted by \(M_Q(U,W)\) has one endpoint in some
\(U\cap V_\tau\) and the other in \(W\cap V_{\bar\tau}\).  The total weight of
all possible ordered cross pairs of this type is
\[
\sum_{\tau\in\{0,1\}^m}A_\tau B_{\bar\tau}.
\]
The actual lower edge set may be a strict subgraph of these permitted pairs,
so (2) follows.  Grouping the terms for \(\tau\) and \(\bar\tau\) gives the
unordered form (1).

Normalize
\[
p_\tau=\frac{A_\tau}{A},
\qquad
q_\tau=\frac{B_\tau}{B}.
\]
Then \(p\) and \(q\) are probability vectors, and (2), (3) imply
\[
\sum_\tau p_\tau q_{\bar\tau}\ge\lambda.
\tag{6}
\]
Set \(r_\tau=q_{\bar\tau}\).  Let
\[
P=\max_\tau p_\tau.
\]
Because \(\sum_\tau p_\tau r_\tau\le P\), (6) gives \(P\ge\lambda\).

First suppose \(P>1/2\).  Choose any \(\tau^*\) with
\[
p_{\tau^*}=P,
\]
and put \(R=r_{\tau^*}=q_{\bar\tau^*}\).  As in the near-full corridor
argument [[mrw-8a0c228a0166]],
\[
\sum_\tau p_\tau r_\tau
=
PR+\sum_{\tau\ne\tau^*}p_\tau r_\tau
\le
PR+(1-P)(1-R).
\tag{7}
\]
If \(R<\lambda\), then, because \(P\ge\lambda\ge1/2\),
\[
PR+(1-P)(1-R)
<
P\lambda+(1-P)(1-\lambda)
=
\lambda+(1-P)(1-2\lambda)
\le
\lambda,
\]
contradicting (6).  Hence \(R\ge\lambda\).  Multiplying
\[
p_{\tau^*}\ge\lambda,
\qquad
q_{\bar\tau^*}=R\ge\lambda
\]
by \(A\) and \(B\) proves (4).

It remains to handle the endpoint-tie case \(P=1/2\).  Then
\(\lambda=1/2\), since \(P\ge\lambda\ge1/2\).  Also
\[
\sum_\tau p_\tau r_\tau\le P=\frac12
\]
and (6) forces equality.  Equality in this bound means that \(r\) is supported
on the set
\[
S=\{\tau:p_\tau=1/2\}.
\]
The set \(S\) has at most two elements.  Choose \(\tau^*\in S\) with
\(r_{\tau^*}\) maximal.  Then \(p_{\tau^*}=1/2\) and
\[
r_{\tau^*}\ge\frac12.
\]
Since \(q_{\bar\tau^*}=r_{\tau^*}\), (4) follows in this remaining case.

For equality, apply (4) with \(\lambda=1\).  Then
\[
A_{\tau^*}=A,
\qquad
B_{\bar\tau^*}=B,
\]
so all other ancestor-signature classes on the corresponding sides have zero
weight.  Since \(AB\) is the total weight of all possible pairs across the
positive side masses, equality \(M_Q(U,W)=AB\) also forces every positive
product pair across the two supports to be an actual lower edge; otherwise a
missing pair would subtract a positive amount.

## Notes

- This is the quantitative version of [[mrw-49eaa53e7ffe]].  It applies before
  exact completeness: a near-complete lower corridor can lose at most
  \(1-\lambda\) side-weight outside one inherited complementary ancestor
  signature pair.
- The result is still local to a chosen lower corridor \(U|W\) and a fixed
  list of ancestor upper complete-bipartite cuts.  It does not prove a global
  positive-mass obstruction, \(M_{P_k}(\theta)\to0\), \(U_k(\theta)\to0\), or
  an \(R_P(\theta)\) lift.
- The next global step must prove that positive high-support mass creates
  incompatible near-purity requirements across many such corridors, or else
  construct a coherent signature-tree assembly and test it against every full
  pair-link interval.
