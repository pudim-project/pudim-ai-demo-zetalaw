# Proposition: Top-cover section pileup is inverse-difference cover energy

## Statement
Let \(g\) be finite and let \(\pi_g\) be a product probability law on \(2^g\)
with coordinate probabilities \(0<q_b<1\).  For \(x,y\subseteq g\), define
the inverse-difference cover weight
\[
\Delta_g(x,y)
=
\mathbf 1_{x\cup y=g}\prod_{b\in x\triangle y}q_b^{-1}.
\]
Then the top-cover kernel from `mrw-ce7f13a668ed` satisfies
\[
\kappa_g(x,y)=\pi_g(x)\pi_g(y)\Delta_g(x,y).
\]

Consequently, if \(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q\subseteq2^g\) have positive
\(\pi_g\)-masses, then
\[
K_g(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
:=
\frac{\Phi_g(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)}
{\pi_g(\mathcal P)\pi_g(\mathcal Q)}
=
\mathbb E[\Delta_g(X,Y)\mid X\in\mathcal P,\ Y\in\mathcal Q],
\]
where \(X,Y\) are independent \(\pi_g\)-random subsets.  In particular, if
\(L\ge0\) and
\[
K_g(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)>L,
\]
then some \(x\in\mathcal P\), \(y\in\mathcal Q\) satisfy
\[
x\cup y=g,
\qquad
\prod_{b\in x\triangle y}q_b^{-1}>L.
\]

Let
\[
\operatorname{cov}_g(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
=
\mathbb P(X\cup Y=g\mid X\in\mathcal P,\ Y\in\mathcal Q).
\]
If every covering pair in \(\mathcal P\times\mathcal Q\) satisfies
\[
\prod_{b\in x\triangle y}q_b\ge\alpha>0,
\]
then
\[
K_g(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
\le
\alpha^{-1}\operatorname{cov}_g(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q).
\]
Therefore, if \(c>0\), \(L>0\),
\[
\operatorname{cov}_g(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)\le c,
\qquad
K_g(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)>L,
\]
then some covering pair satisfies
\[
\prod_{b\in x\triangle y}q_b<c/L.
\]

The ordered distinct repeated-parent version is as follows.  For
\(\mathcal P\subseteq2^g\), define
\[
Z_{\mathcal P}^{\ne}
=
\sum_{\substack{x,y\in\mathcal P\\x\ne y}}\pi_g(x)\pi_g(y).
\]
If \(Z_{\mathcal P}^{\ne}>0\), then
\[
K_g^{\ne}(\mathcal P)
:=
\frac{\Phi_g^{\ne}(\mathcal P)}{Z_{\mathcal P}^{\ne}}
\]
is the expectation of \(\Delta_g(X,Y)\) under the ordered distinct
conditional law on \(X,Y\in\mathcal P\), \(X\ne Y\).  With
\[
\operatorname{cov}_g^{\ne}(\mathcal P)
=
\frac{
\sum_{\substack{x,y\in\mathcal P\\x\ne y,\ x\cup y=g}}
\pi_g(x)\pi_g(y)
}{Z_{\mathcal P}^{\ne}},
\]
the same extraction and cover-probability bounds hold for ordered distinct
covering pairs.  If \(Z_{\mathcal P}^{\ne}=0\), no repeated-parent density is
asserted.

Combining with `mrw-ce7f13a668ed`, if fixed-child pileup satisfies
\[
D_{\mathcal A,\mathcal C}(g)\ge L>0
\]
and \(0<\rho\le1\), then either
\[
W_g(\mathcal A,\mathcal C)\ge
\rho\pi_B(\mathcal A)\pi_B(\mathcal C),
\]
or there is an outside core \(h\subseteq B\setminus g\) and traces
\[
x\in\mathcal A_h,\qquad y\in\mathcal C_h
\]
such that
\[
x\cup y=g,
\qquad
\prod_{b\in x\triangle y}q_b^{-1}>L/\rho.
\]
Equivalently, \(a=h\cup x\) and \(c=h\cup y\) satisfy
\[
g\in I_B(a,c),
\qquad
\prod_{b\in a\triangle c}q_b^{-1}>L/\rho.
\]
The same fixed-child consequence holds for the ordered distinct
repeated-parent density \(D_{\mathcal A}^{\ne}(g)\), with \(W_g^{\ne}\) and
\(Z_{\mathcal A}\).

## Proof
For \(x,y\subseteq g\),
\[
\pi_g(x)\pi_g(y)
=
\prod_{b\in x\cap y}q_b^2
\prod_{b\in x\triangle y}q_b(1-q_b)
\prod_{b\in g\setminus(x\cup y)}(1-q_b)^2.
\]
If \(x\cup y\ne g\), both \(\Delta_g(x,y)\) and \(\kappa_g(x,y)\) vanish.
If \(x\cup y=g\), then
\[
x\triangle y=g\setminus(x\cap y).
\]
Multiplying by
\[
\prod_{b\in x\triangle y}q_b^{-1}
\]
therefore gives
\[
\pi_g(x)\pi_g(y)\Delta_g(x,y)
=
\prod_{b\in x\cap y}q_b^2
\prod_{b\in g\setminus(x\cap y)}(1-q_b)
=
\kappa_g(x,y).
\]

Summing this identity over \(x\in\mathcal P\) and \(y\in\mathcal Q\), then
dividing by the positive mass product
\(\pi_g(\mathcal P)\pi_g(\mathcal Q)\), gives the displayed expectation
identity for \(K_g(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)\).  Since
\(\Delta_g\ge0\), if the conditional expectation is greater than \(L\ge0\),
at least one conditional atom has \(\Delta_g(x,y)>L\).  Such an atom must be
a covering pair because non-covering pairs have \(\Delta_g=0\).  This proves
the energetic-pair extraction.

For the cover-probability bound, write
\[
q_{x\triangle y}:=\prod_{b\in x\triangle y}q_b.
\]
If every covering pair has \(q_{x\triangle y}\ge\alpha>0\), then
\[
\Delta_g(x,y)
\le
\alpha^{-1}\mathbf 1_{x\cup y=g}.
\]
Averaging under the conditional law on
\(\mathcal P\times\mathcal Q\) gives
\[
K_g(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
\le
\alpha^{-1}\operatorname{cov}_g(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q).
\]
If \(\operatorname{cov}_g(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)\le c\), \(c>0\), and
\(K_g(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)>L>0\), then the negation of the claimed
tiny-difference conclusion would give
\[
q_{x\triangle y}\ge c/L
\]
for every covering pair.  Applying the preceding inequality with
\(\alpha=c/L\) gives
\[
K_g(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
\le
(L/c)\operatorname{cov}_g(\mathcal P,\mathcal Q)
\le L,
\]
contradiction.

For the repeated-parent version, use the ordered conditional probability
measure
\[
\frac{\pi_g(x)\pi_g(y)}{Z_{\mathcal P}^{\ne}}
\]
on ordered distinct pairs \(x,y\in\mathcal P\), \(x\ne y\).  The same
pointwise identity for \(\kappa_g\) gives the expectation identity for
\(K_g^{\ne}\), and the extraction and cover-probability bounds repeat
verbatim with \(\operatorname{cov}_g^{\ne}\).

For the fixed-child consequence, apply the common-core/top-cover split in
`mrw-ce7f13a668ed`.  If the common-core alternative fails, that proposition
supplies a section of positive denominator with
\[
K_g(\mathcal A_h,\mathcal C_h)>L/\rho.
\]
The extraction just proved gives traces \(x,y\) in that section with
\[
x\cup y=g,
\qquad
\prod_{b\in x\triangle y}q_b^{-1}>L/\rho.
\]
The fixed-child normal form has
\[
a=h\cup x,\qquad c=h\cup y,
\]
so \(g\in I_B(a,c)\) and
\[
a\triangle c=x\triangle y.
\]
This proves the cross-family fixed-child consequence.  The ordered distinct
case is identical, using the \(D_{\mathcal A}^{\ne}\), \(W_g^{\ne}\), and
\(Z_{\mathcal A}\) form of `mrw-ce7f13a668ed`.

## Notes
- This is a local diagnostic proposition, not terminal Erdos 536 evidence.
- In the prime-biased specialization \(q_b=1/p_b\), the extracted energetic
  pair satisfies
  \[
  \prod_{b\in x\triangle y}p_b>L,
  \]
  or \(>L/\rho\) in the fixed-child consequence.
- The proposition extracts one energetic pair or one tiny-difference witness;
  it does not lower-bound the mass or number of such pairs.
- \(g=\emptyset\) is harmless: the only cross-family covering pair is
  \(x=y=\emptyset\), while the ordered distinct denominator is zero.
- If \(g=B\), the fixed-child split has no outside-core averaging.
- Oracle accepted the proof after adding \(L\ge0\) to extraction,
  \(\alpha>0\), \(c>0\), and \(L>0\) to the tiny-difference alternative, and
  writing the ordered distinct cover probability explicitly.  Scout returned
  the same algebraic identity as its first candidate solution and was ingested
  raw-only before local promotion.
