# Theorem: Two-prime row-column fiber bound for Erdos 536

## Statement

Let \(f(N)\) be the largest size of a set \(A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}\) with no three distinct elements \(a,b,c\in A\) satisfying
\[
[a,b]=[a,c]=[b,c].
\]
Then
\[
f(N)\le N-\left\lfloor\frac N6\right\rfloor.
\]
In particular,
\[
f(N)\le \frac56N+O(1).
\]

More generally, if \(A\subseteq[1,N]\) is lcm-triangle-free and \(r\) is coprime to \(6\), then the fiber
\[
S_r=
\{(i,j)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2:\ r2^i3^j\in A\}
\]
inside
\[
D_r=
\{(i,j)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2:\ r2^i3^j\le N\}
\]
has size at most
\[
R_r+C_r-1,
\]
where
\[
R_r=\#\{j\ge0:\ r3^j\le N\},
\qquad
C_r=\#\{i\ge0:\ r2^i\le N\}.
\]

## Proof

First prove the grid lemma.  Let \(D\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2\) be finite, and let \(S\subseteq D\) contain no three distinct points \(x,y,z\) such that
\[
x\vee y=x\vee z=y\vee z,
\]
where \(\vee\) denotes coordinatewise maximum.  Suppose \(x=(i,j)\in S\) is neither the leftmost selected point in its row nor the bottommost selected point in its column.  Then there exist
\[
y=(i',j)\in S,\quad i'<i,
\qquad
z=(i,j')\in S,\quad j'<j.
\]
These three points are distinct and satisfy
\[
x\vee y=x,\qquad x\vee z=x,\qquad y\vee z=x,
\]
contradicting the assumption on \(S\).  Hence every selected point is either row-leftmost or column-bottommost.

If \(S\ne\varnothing\), choose a selected point minimizing \(i+j\).  It is both row-leftmost and column-bottommost.  Therefore, if \(R\) is the number of nonempty rows of \(D\) and \(C\) is the number of nonempty columns of \(D\),
\[
|S|\le R+C-1.
\]
If \(S=\varnothing\), the same inequality is trivial for every nonempty \(D\).

Now let \(A\subseteq[1,N]\) be lcm-triangle-free.  For each \(r\le N\) with \((r,6)=1\), form the fiber \(S_r\) above.  If \(S_r\) contained three distinct points with equal pairwise coordinatewise maximum, then the corresponding integers \(r2^i3^j\) would have equal pairwise lcm: the \(2\)- and \(3\)-adic exponents agree by the coordinatewise maximum condition, and all primes other than \(2,3\) occur with the same exponents from the common residual \(r\).  This would be an lcm triangle in \(A\).  Thus the grid lemma applies to \(S_r\).

The downset \(D_r\) has
\[
R_r=\#\{j\ge0:\ r3^j\le N\}
\]
nonempty rows and
\[
C_r=\#\{i\ge0:\ r2^i\le N\}
\]
nonempty columns.  Therefore
\[
|S_r|\le R_r+C_r-1.
\]
Summing over all \(r\le N\) with \((r,6)=1\) gives
\[
|A|
=
\sum_{\substack{r\le N\\(r,6)=1}}|S_r|
\le
\sum_{\substack{r\le N\\(r,6)=1}}(R_r+C_r-1).
\]
The last sum counts exactly the integers \(n\le N\) with \(v_2(n)=0\) or \(v_3(n)=0\): each term \(R_r\) counts the axis points \(r3^j\), each term \(C_r\) counts the axis points \(r2^i\), and the subtraction removes the overlap \(r\).  These are precisely the integers not divisible by \(6\).  Hence
\[
|A|\le N-\left\lfloor\frac N6\right\rfloor.
\]
Taking the maximum over \(A\) proves the theorem.

## Notes

- This is stronger than all disjoint-packing bounds currently promoted in the wiki, but it is still only a constant-density upper bound.
- The proof uses overlapping constraints inside each \((2,3)\)-free fiber; it is not merely a disjoint forbidden-triple packing.
- A direct higher-dimensional analogue is nontrivial because in three or more prime coordinates, an lcm triangle need not contain its common coordinatewise maximum.
