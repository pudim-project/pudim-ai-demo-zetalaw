# Proposition: Disjoint lcm-triangle packing upper bound for Erdos 536

## Statement

Let \(f(N)\) be the largest size of \(A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}\) with no three distinct elements \(a,b,c\in A\) satisfying
\[
[a,b]=[a,c]=[b,c].
\]
Then the elementary one-template packing gives
\[
f(N)\le \frac{35}{36}N+O(1).
\]
The same method gives the stronger bounds
\[
f(N)\le \frac{17}{18}N+O(1)
\]
and
\[
f(N)\le \frac{11}{12}N+O((\log N)^2).
\]

More precisely, if
\[
R_N=\{r\in\mathbb N:\ N/12<r\le N/6,\ (r,6)=1\},
\]
then every such \(A\) omits at least one integer from each of the pairwise disjoint triples
\[
\{2r,3r,6r\}\qquad(r\in R_N),
\]
and
\[
|R_N|=\frac{N}{36}+O(1).
\]

## Proof

For each \(r\ge1\), the three integers \(2r,3r,6r\) are distinct and satisfy
\[
[2r,3r]=[2r,6r]=[3r,6r]=6r.
\]
Therefore any lcm-triangle-free set \(A\) contains at most two elements from each triple.

It remains to check disjointness for \(r\in R_N\).  Suppose two entries from triples indexed by \(r,s\in R_N\) are equal:
\[
\lambda r=\mu s,\qquad \lambda,\mu\in\{2,3,6\}.
\]
If \(\lambda=\mu\), then \(r=s\).  If \(\lambda\ne\mu\), then after cancelling the common factor of \(\lambda\) and \(\mu\), one obtains one of the divisibility requirements
\[
2\mid r,\qquad 3\mid r,\qquad 2\mid s,\qquad 3\mid s.
\]
This is impossible because \((r,6)=(s,6)=1\).  Thus distinct \(r\)'s give disjoint triples.

The condition \(r\le N/6\) guarantees \(6r\le N\), so every triple lies in \([1,N]\).  Since a lcm-triangle-free set omits at least one point from each disjoint triple,
\[
|A|\le N-|R_N|.
\]
The integers coprime to \(6\) have density \(\varphi(6)/6=1/3\), hence
\[
|R_N|
=
\frac13\left(\frac{N}{6}-\frac{N}{12}\right)+O(1)
=
\frac{N}{36}+O(1).
\]
Consequently
\[
|A|\le N-\frac{N}{36}+O(1)=\frac{35}{36}N+O(1),
\]
and maximizing over \(A\) gives the first asserted bound for \(f(N)\).

The lower cutoff \(r>N/12\) is not needed for disjointness.  Taking instead
\[
1\le r\le N/6,\qquad (r,6)=1,
\]
gives
\[
\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{N}{6}+O(1)=\frac{N}{18}+O(1)
\]
pairwise disjoint lcm triangles, hence
\[
f(N)\le \frac{17}{18}N+O(1).
\]

For the two-prime grid refinement, fix \(r\) coprime to \(6\) and even integers \(i,j\ge0\).  The triple
\[
\left\{
2^{i+1}3^j r,\,
2^i3^{j+1}r,\,
2^{i+1}3^{j+1}r
\right\}
\]
has equal pairwise lcm
\[
2^{i+1}3^{j+1}r.
\]
The triples with \(i,j\) even and \(2^{i+1}3^{j+1}r\le N\) are pairwise disjoint.  Indeed, after writing each member as \(2^\alpha3^\beta r\) with \((r,6)=1\), equality of two members forces equality of \(\alpha,\beta\), and then equality of the residual factor \(r\).  The three roles in one cell have parity types
\[
(\alpha,\beta)\equiv(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)\pmod2,
\]
so different roles cannot overlap; identical roles force the same even pair \(i,j\).

The number of such triples is
\[
\sum_{u,v\ge0}
\#\left\{r\le \frac{N}{2^{2u+1}3^{2v+1}}:\ (r,6)=1\right\}.
\]
Since
\[
\#\{r\le X:(r,6)=1\}=\frac{X}{3}+O(1),
\]
and only \(O((\log N)^2)\) pairs \((u,v)\) contribute, the count is
\[
\frac{N}{3}
\sum_{u,v\ge0}\frac{1}{2^{2u+1}3^{2v+1}}
+O((\log N)^2).
\]
The product of the two geometric sums is
\[
\left(\frac{1/2}{1-1/4}\right)
\left(\frac{1/3}{1-1/9}\right)
=\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{3}{8}
=\frac14.
\]
Therefore there are
\[
\frac{N}{12}+O((\log N)^2)
\]
disjoint lcm triangles in \([1,N]\), and every lcm-triangle-free set omits at least one point from each.  Hence
\[
f(N)\le N-\frac{N}{12}+O((\log N)^2)
=\frac{11}{12}N+O((\log N)^2).
\]

## Notes

- This does not solve \(f(N)=o(N)\).  It supplies a locally audited constant-density upper bound and a packing invariant to improve in future cycles.
- The source page for Erdos #536 records a comment-level bound of the form \(f(N)\le(221/225+o(1))N\).  The proof here is self-contained and should be treated as a local result independent of that comment.
- The \(11/12\) bound is superseded as the best local constant by the dyadic multiscale proposition, but this node remains the base unit-corner construction.
