# Proposition: Signature fragmentation bounds nested bipartite lower slices

## Statement

Let \(P\) be a finite set and let \(\mathcal F\subseteq2^P\) be
pair-link-free.  For \(R\subseteq P\), write
\[
G_R^\mathcal F=(P\setminus R,E_R^\mathcal F),
\qquad
\{x,y\}\in E_R^\mathcal F
\Longleftrightarrow
R\cup\{x,y\}\in\mathcal F.
\]

Assume \(m\ge1\), \(Q\subseteq R_i\subseteq P\) for
\(1\le i\le m\), and \(V\subseteq P\setminus\bigcup_i R_i\).  Suppose that
for each \(i\),
\[
V=X_i\sqcup Y_i,
\qquad
X_i,Y_i\ne\varnothing,
\]
and \(G_{R_i}^\mathcal F\) contains the complete bipartite graph
\(K_{X_i,Y_i}\).  Define the signature of \(v\in V\) by
\[
\sigma(v)=(\mathbf 1_{v\in X_i})_{i=1}^m\in\{0,1\}^m.
\]
For \(\tau\in\{0,1\}^m\), put
\[
V_\tau=\{v\in V:\sigma(v)=\tau\},
\qquad
\bar\tau=\mathbf 1-\tau.
\]

Let \(a_v\ge0\) be arbitrary weights on \(V\), and define
\[
W_\tau=\sum_{v\in V_\tau}a_v,
\qquad
W=\sum_{\tau\in\{0,1\}^m}W_\tau.
\]
For the lower slice edge mass on \(V\), set
\[
M_Q(V)
=
\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E_Q^\mathcal F\cap\binom V2}a_u a_v .
\]
Then
\[
M_Q(V)
\le
\sum_{\{\tau,\bar\tau\}} W_\tau W_{\bar\tau},
\tag{1}
\]
where the sum is over unordered complementary signature pairs.

Consequently, if \(W>0\) and
\[
\rho=\max_{\tau\in\{0,1\}^m}\frac{W_\tau}{W},
\]
then
\[
M_Q(V)\le \frac{\rho}{2}W^2.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, if \(\delta\ge0\) and \(M_Q(V)\ge\delta W^2\), then some
signature class has weight at least \(2\delta W\).  Also, since there are
\(2^{m-1}\) unordered complementary signature pairs, some pair satisfies
\[
W_\tau W_{\bar\tau}
\ge
\frac{\delta}{2^{m-1}}W^2.
\tag{3}
\]

## Proof

By [[mrw-fced7420b905]], every lower edge
\[
\{u,v\}\in E_Q^\mathcal F\cap\binom V2
\]
connects complementary signatures:
\[
\sigma(u)=\mathbf 1-\sigma(v).
\]
Therefore the lower edge set on \(V\) is contained in the union of the
complete bipartite corridors
\[
V_\tau\times V_{\bar\tau}
\]
over unordered complementary signature pairs.  The weight of all possible
pairs across one such corridor is \(W_\tau W_{\bar\tau}\).  Summing over
the unordered complementary pairs and discarding the fact that not every
possible cross pair must be an actual lower edge gives (1).

For (2), use (1) and count each unordered complementary pair twice:
\[
\sum_{\{\tau,\bar\tau\}} W_\tau W_{\bar\tau}
=
\frac12\sum_{\tau\in\{0,1\}^m} W_\tau W_{\bar\tau}
\le
\frac12\sum_{\tau\in\{0,1\}^m} W_\tau\,\rho W
=
\frac{\rho}{2}W^2.
\]
If \(\delta\ge0\) and \(M_Q(V)\ge\delta W^2\), then (2) implies
\(\rho\ge2\delta\), so some signature class has weight at least
\(2\delta W\).

Finally, (1) writes \(M_Q(V)\) below a sum of \(2^{m-1}\) nonnegative
complementary-pair products.  If all of those products were smaller than
\(\delta W^2/2^{m-1}\), then \(M_Q(V)<\delta W^2\).  Thus (3) follows by
averaging.

## Notes

- This is a weighted bookkeeping consequence of signature coherence, not a
  terminal mass theorem.
- Fragmentation of signatures gives a quantitative obstruction: if every
  signature class has \(W_\tau\le\rho W\), then the lower slice edge mass on
  \(V\) is at most \(\rho W^2/2\).
- Conversely, large lower edge mass forces a heavy complementary signature
  corridor.  The next global step must show that positive high-support mass
  creates enough independent upper cuts to fragment signatures, or else
  reduce the surviving mass to a stable corridor that can be tested against
  the fixed complete-bipartite blow-up quarantine from [[mrw-c7c76faed872]].
- No claim is made here that \(M_{P_k}(\theta)\to0\), that
  \(U_k(\theta)\to0\), or that the exponent-grid residual
  \(R_P(\theta)\) is controlled.
