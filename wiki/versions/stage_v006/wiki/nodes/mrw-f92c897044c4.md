# Proposition: Union-tail criterion for Erdos residue-class logarithmic density

## Statement

Use the notation of [[wiki/nodes/mrw-ba29cdf1fd30|Finite-shadow reduction for Erdos residue-class logarithmic density]].  For
\[
E_N=B_N\setminus B
\]
define the union-tail majorant
\[
U_N=
\limsup_{x\to\infty}
\frac1{\log x}
\sum_{\substack{i>N\\ n_i<x}}
\frac{1+\log(x/n_i)}{n_i}.
\]
If
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}U_N=0,
\]
then \(B\) has logarithmic density
\[
\delta=\lim_{N\to\infty}\delta_N.
\]
In particular, this holds whenever
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n_i}<\infty.
\]

The criterion is sufficient but not necessary.  There are systems with \(B\) of logarithmic density \(1/2\) and \(E_N=\varnothing\) for all sufficiently large \(N\), while \(U_N=\infty\) for every fixed \(N\).

## Proof

For \(q\ge1\), a residue class \(a\pmod q\), and \(x>q\), we first record the elementary bound
\[
\sum_{\substack{q\le m<x\\ m\equiv a\pmod q}}\frac1m
\le
\frac{1+\log(x/q)}{q}.
\]
Indeed, each interval \([\ell q,(\ell+1)q)\), \(\ell\ge1\), contains at most one integer in the fixed residue class, and any such integer contributes at most \(1/(\ell q)\).  Hence the sum is at most
\[
\frac1q\sum_{\ell\le x/q}\frac1\ell
\le
\frac{1+\log(x/q)}q.
\]

For fixed \(N\),
\[
E_N\subseteq
\bigcup_{\substack{i>N\\ n_i<x}}
\{m:\ n_i\le m<x,\ m\equiv a_i\pmod {n_i}\}
\]
inside the interval \(m<x\).  Therefore
\[
\mu_x(E_N)
\le
\frac1{\log x}
\sum_{\substack{i>N\\ n_i<x}}
\sum_{\substack{n_i\le m<x\\ m\equiv a_i\pmod {n_i}}}\frac1m
\le
\frac1{\log x}
\sum_{\substack{i>N\\ n_i<x}}
\frac{1+\log(x/n_i)}{n_i}.
\]
Taking upper limits in \(x\) gives
\[
\Theta_N:=
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\mu_x(E_N)
\le U_N.
\]
Thus \(U_N\to0\) implies \(\Theta_N\to0\), and the tail-defect criterion in [[wiki/nodes/mrw-ba29cdf1fd30|Finite-shadow reduction for Erdos residue-class logarithmic density]] proves that \(B\) has logarithmic density \(\delta\).

If \(\sum_i1/n_i<\infty\), then for \(x\ge e\) and \(n_i<x\),
\[
\frac{1+\log(x/n_i)}{\log x}\le 2.
\]
Consequently
\[
U_N\le 2\sum_{i>N}\frac1{n_i},
\]
which tends to \(0\).  This proves the summable-reciprocal corollary.

It remains only to justify the non-necessity claim.  Let
\[
n_i=2i,\qquad a_i\equiv0\pmod {2i}.
\]
The first condition excludes exactly the even integers once \(m\ge2\), and every later excluded progression is contained in the even integers.  Hence \(B\) is the set of odd positive integers and has logarithmic density \(1/2\).  In fact, after the first condition no later condition removes any point of \(B_1\), so \(E_1=\varnothing\).

However, for every fixed \(N\),
\[
U_N\ge
\limsup_{x\to\infty}
\frac1{\log x}
\sum_{N<i\le \sqrt{x}/2}
\frac{\log(x/(2i))}{2i}.
\]
For \(N<i\le\sqrt{x}/2\), one has \(\log(x/(2i))\ge \frac12\log x\), and therefore the last expression is bounded below by
\[
\limsup_{x\to\infty}
\frac14
\sum_{N<i\le \sqrt{x}/2}\frac1i
=\infty.
\]
Thus \(U_N=\infty\) for every fixed \(N\), even though the actual tail defect is already zero.  The union-tail criterion therefore does not capture the overlap structure needed for the full Erdos problem.
