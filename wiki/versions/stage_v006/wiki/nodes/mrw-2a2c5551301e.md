# Proposition: Tilted-thinning boundary criterion for lower-shadow candidates

## Statement

Let \(P\) be a finite set of primes and let \(\nu_P\) be the product law on
\(2^P\) with \(\nu_P(p\in A)=1/p\).  For \(1/2\le\tau<1\), define the tilted
law \(\nu_{P,\tau}\) by
\[
\nu_{P,\tau}(p\in C)=\frac1{\tau p}.
\]
Sample \(C\sim\nu_{P,\tau}\), and then form \(A\subseteq C\) by retaining each
element of \(C\) independently with probability \(\tau\).  Then \(A\sim\nu_P\).

Assume the following \(r\)-biased intersecting trace bound: for every finite
ground set \(C\), every pairwise-intersecting family
\(\mathcal I\subseteq2^C\), and every \(0\le r\le1/2\), the \(r\)-biased product
measure of \(\mathcal I\) is at most \(r\).

If \(\mathcal F\subseteq2^P\) is lower-shadow union-cover-free, then
\[
\Pr(A\in\mathcal F,\ C\in\mathcal F)
\le
(1-\tau)\nu_{P,\tau}(\mathcal F)
+
\mathbb E_{\nu_{P,\tau}}\!\left[1_{\mathcal F}(C)\tau^{|C|}\right].
\]
Consequently,
\[
\Pr(A\in\mathcal F,\ C\in(\uparrow\mathcal F)\setminus\mathcal F)
\ge
\nu_P(\mathcal F)
-
(1-\tau)\nu_{P,\tau}(\mathcal F)
-
\mathbb E_{\nu_{P,\tau}}\!\left[1_{\mathcal F}(C)\tau^{|C|}\right].
\]
In particular, if \(0<\theta<1\), \(S_P=\sum_{p\in P}1/p\), and
\(\mathcal F\subseteq H_{P,\theta}=\{S: |S|>\theta S_P\}\), then
\[
\Pr(A\in\mathcal F,\ C\in(\uparrow\mathcal F)\setminus\mathcal F)
\ge
\nu_P(\mathcal F)
-
\left(1-\tau+\tau^{\theta S_P}\right)\nu_{P,\tau}(\mathcal F).
\]
Thus, along \(P_k=\{p_1,\ldots,p_k\}\) with \(S_{P_k}\to\infty\), every
lower-shadow union-cover-free family
\(\mathcal F_k\subseteq H_{P_k,\theta}\) satisfying
\(\nu_{P_k}(\mathcal F_k)\ge\eta>0\) has positive tilted upward-boundary
leakage for every fixed \(\tau\in(1-\eta,1)\) once \(k\) is large.  For example,
if \(1-\tau<\eta/4\), then the leakage is at least \(\eta/2\) for all large
\(k\).

## Proof

The marginal law of \(A\) is \(\nu_P\), because a coordinate \(p\) is first
included in \(C\) with probability \(1/(\tau p)\) and then retained with
probability \(\tau\).

Fix \(C\in\mathcal F\).  By the deletion-trace equivalence in
[[mrw-cc4f876149b7]], the family
\[
\mathcal D_{\mathcal F}(C)
=
\{D\subseteq C:\ D\ne\varnothing,\ C\setminus D\in\mathcal F\}
\]
is pairwise intersecting, because \(\mathcal F\) is lower-shadow
union-cover-free.  Conditional on \(C\), write \(D=C\setminus A\).  Then
\(D\) is distributed by the product law on \(C\) with inclusion probability
\(1-\tau\).  Since \(1-\tau\le1/2\), the assumed biased intersecting trace
bound gives
\[
\Pr(D\in\mathcal D_{\mathcal F}(C)\mid C)\le 1-\tau.
\]
The remaining way to have \(A\in\mathcal F\) while \(C\in\mathcal F\) and
\(D\notin\mathcal D_{\mathcal F}(C)\) is the undeleted case \(D=\varnothing\),
which has probability \(\tau^{|C|}\).  Hence
\[
\Pr(A\in\mathcal F\mid C)\le 1-\tau+\tau^{|C|}
\qquad(C\in\mathcal F).
\]
Multiplying by \(1_{\mathcal F}(C)\) and integrating over
\(C\sim\nu_{P,\tau}\) proves the self-overlap bound.

For the boundary inequality, use the upward-boundary identity from
[[mrw-4a98da3d7f40]], which remains valid for the tilted coupling:
if \(A\in\mathcal F\) and \(A\subseteq C\), then \(C\in\uparrow\mathcal F\), so
\[
\nu_P(\mathcal F)
=
\Pr(A\in\mathcal F,\ C\in\mathcal F)
+
\Pr(A\in\mathcal F,\ C\in(\uparrow\mathcal F)\setminus\mathcal F).
\]
Subtracting the self-overlap upper bound gives the displayed lower bound.

If \(\mathcal F\subseteq H_{P,\theta}\), then \(C\in\mathcal F\) implies
\(|C|>\theta S_P\), and therefore \(\tau^{|C|}\le\tau^{\theta S_P}\).  This
gives the high-support specialization.  Finally, for \(P_k\) with
\(S_{P_k}\to\infty\), fixed \(0<\theta<1\), and fixed \(\tau<1\), the factor
\(\tau^{\theta S_{P_k}}\) tends to zero.  Since
\(\nu_{P_k,\tau}(\mathcal F_k)\le1\), if
\(\nu_{P_k}(\mathcal F_k)\ge\eta\) and \(1-\tau<\eta/4\), then the leakage is
at least \(\eta-(1-\tau)-\tau^{\theta S_{P_k}}\ge\eta/2\) for all large \(k\).

## Notes

- At \(\tau=1/2\), this recovers the fair-thinning shape of
  [[mrw-c228258e6ab4]], where the intersecting trace bound is available from
  complement pairing.  The point of the tilted form is that \(\tau\) can be
  chosen near \(1\), making the allowed lower-shadow self-overlap coefficient
  \(1-\tau\) arbitrarily small.
- The result is conditional in the precise sense stated above: the only
  nonlocal input is the standard \(r\)-biased intersecting-family bound.
- This does not prove the lower-shadow union-cover theorem.  It sharpens the
  obstruction: a positive-mass counterexample must be nonmonotone,
  tail-sensitive, and boundary-heavy even under a near-identity sprouting
  coupling.
