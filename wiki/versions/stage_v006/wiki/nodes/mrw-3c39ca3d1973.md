# Proposition: Pair-link shadow criterion for biased squarefree residuals

## Statement

Let \(P\) be a finite set of primes, and let \(\nu_P\) be the product probability measure on \(2^P\) with
\[
\nu_P(p\in S)=\frac1p.
\]
For \(A,B\subseteq P\), define the pair-completion interval
\[
I(A,B)=\{C\subseteq P:\ A\triangle B\subseteq C\subseteq A\cup B\},
\]
and define
\[
I^\circ(A,B)=I(A,B)\setminus\{A,B\}.
\]
Then \(A,B,C\subseteq P\) satisfy the coordinate cosunflower condition, meaning no coordinate belongs to exactly one of \(A,B,C\), if and only if \(C\in I(A,B)\).  Consequently, if \(A,B,C\) are pairwise distinct, then they form a distinct squarefree cosunflower if and only if \(A\ne B\) and \(C\in I^\circ(A,B)\).

Moreover,
\[
\nu_P(I(A,B))
=
\prod_{p\in A\triangle B}\frac1p
\prod_{p\notin A\cup B}\left(1-\frac1p\right).
\]

For a family \(\mathcal F\subseteq2^P\), define its pair-link shadow by
\[
\mathcal L_P(\mathcal F)
=
\bigcup_{\substack{A,B\in\mathcal F\\A\ne B}} I^\circ(A,B).
\]
Then \(\mathcal F\) is squarefree-cosunflower-free if and only if
\[
\mathcal F\cap\mathcal L_P(\mathcal F)=\emptyset.
\]

Finally, if \(\mathcal F\) is squarefree-cosunflower-free and \(C\in\mathcal F\), then there are no two distinct \(A,B\in\mathcal F\cap2^C\), with \(A,B\ne C\), such that
\[
A\cup B=C.
\]
In particular,
\[
\left|\{p\in C:\ C\setminus\{p\}\in\mathcal F\}\right|\le1.
\]

## Proof

Fix \(A,B\subseteq P\).  For each \(p\in P\), the coordinate condition that \(p\) is not present in exactly one of \(A,B,C\) gives three cases.

If \(p\in A\triangle B\), then exactly one of \(A,B\) contains \(p\), so \(C\) must contain \(p\).  If \(p\notin A\cup B\), then neither \(A\) nor \(B\) contains \(p\), so \(C\) must omit \(p\).  If \(p\in A\cap B\), then \(C\) is unrestricted at \(p\).  These coordinatewise conditions are exactly
\[
A\triangle B\subseteq C\subseteq A\cup B,
\]
so \(A,B,C\) satisfy the coordinate cosunflower condition if and only if \(C\in I(A,B)\).  If \(A,B,C\) are required to be distinct, then \(A\ne B\) and \(C\ne A,B\), which is exactly the additional condition \(C\in I^\circ(A,B)\) for the ordered pair \(A,B\).

The measure formula follows from independence under \(\nu_P\).  Coordinates in \(A\triangle B\) are forced to be present and contribute factors \(1/p\).  Coordinates outside \(A\cup B\) are forced to be absent and contribute factors \(1-1/p\).  Coordinates in \(A\cap B\) are free and contribute factor \(1\).  Multiplying these factors gives the displayed formula.

Now let \(\mathcal F\subseteq2^P\).  If \(\mathcal F\) contains a squarefree cosunflower \(A,B,C\) of three distinct members, then \(C\in I(A,B)\), and since \(C\ne A,B\), we have \(C\in I^\circ(A,B)\subseteq\mathcal L_P(\mathcal F)\).  Thus \(\mathcal F\cap\mathcal L_P(\mathcal F)\ne\emptyset\).  Conversely, if \(C\in\mathcal F\cap\mathcal L_P(\mathcal F)\), then for some distinct \(A,B\in\mathcal F\) one has \(C\in I^\circ(A,B)\).  Hence \(A,B,C\) are distinct members of \(\mathcal F\), and the first part shows that they form a squarefree cosunflower.  This proves the shadow criterion.

Finally suppose \(\mathcal F\) is squarefree-cosunflower-free, \(C\in\mathcal F\), and there are distinct \(A,B\in\mathcal F\cap2^C\), with \(A,B\ne C\), such that \(A\cup B=C\).  Then
\[
A\triangle B\subseteq A\cup B=C\subseteq A\cup B,
\]
so \(C\in I(A,B)\).  Since \(A,B,C\) are distinct members of \(\mathcal F\), this contradicts the shadow criterion.  Therefore no such \(A,B\) exist.

For the last claim, if \(p\ne q\in C\) and both \(C\setminus\{p\}\) and \(C\setminus\{q\}\) lie in \(\mathcal F\), then these two distinct proper subsets have union \(C\), contradicting the preceding paragraph.  Hence at most one such one-deletion belongs to \(\mathcal F\).

## Notes

- This proposition does not prove \(M_{P_k}(\theta)\to0\).  It gives the exact pair-link hypergraph formulation of that residual.
- A biased lower-shadow union-cover theorem would be a sufficient route: if for every fixed \(\eta>0\), every high-support family of \(\nu_{P_k}\)-mass at least \(\eta\) contains \(A,B,C\) with \(A,B\subsetneq C\), \(A\ne B\), and \(A\cup B=C\) for all large \(k\), then \(M_{P_k}(\theta)\to0\).
- That route is sufficient but not equivalent to the full pair-link problem, because a pair-link third set \(C\) need not equal \(A\cup B\).
- The proposition is squarefree support-level only.  It does not by itself lift to the exponent-grid prefix-rank residual \(R_P(\theta)\).
