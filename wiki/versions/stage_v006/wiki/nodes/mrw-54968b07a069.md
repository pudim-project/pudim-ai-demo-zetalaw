# Proposition: Product-measure antichain bounds kill max-fiber skeletons

## Statement

Let \(P_k=\{p_1,\ldots,p_k\}\) be the first \(k\) primes and put
\[
q_i=\frac1{p_i},\qquad S_k=\sum_{i\le k}q_i.
\]
Let \(\nu_k\) be the product law on \(2^{[k]}\) with \(\nu_k(i\in S)=q_i\).  For each \(m\le k\), let
\[
\mathcal A_m\subseteq 2^{\{1,\ldots,m-1\}}
\]
be an antichain, and define the max-fiber skeleton
\[
\mathcal F_k=\{X\cup\{m\}: X\in\mathcal A_m,\ 1\le m\le k\}.
\]
Assume the product-measure antichain estimate
\[
\mu(\mathcal A)\le C\left(1+\sum_i r_i(1-r_i)\right)^{-1/2}
\tag{1}
\]
for every antichain \(\mathcal A\) under every Bernoulli product law with coordinate probabilities \(0<r_i\le1/2\).  Then for every fixed \(0\le\theta<1\),
\[
\nu_k\bigl(\mathcal F_k\cap\{|S|>\theta S_k\}\bigr)\to0.
\]

Consequently, under the cited product-measure LYM and anti-concentration theorem, the max-fiber antichain skeletons from [[mrw-2ff2fe94bc57]] cannot give a positive-mass high-support obstruction to the weighted union-free problem [[mrw-55a8d9eddd2e]].

## Proof

Write
\[
T_m=\sum_{i<m}q_i,\qquad V_m=\sum_{i<m}q_i(1-q_i).
\]
Since \(q_i\le1/2\), \(V_m\ge T_m/2\).  Under \(\nu_k\), the contribution of the fiber whose maximum is \(m\) is
\[
w_{m,k}\,
\mu_{m-1}\bigl(\mathcal A_m\cap\{|X|+1>\theta S_k\}\bigr),
\qquad
w_{m,k}=q_m\prod_{i>m}(1-q_i),
\]
where \(\mu_{m-1}\) is the lower-coordinate product law on \(\{1,\ldots,m-1\}\).  The weights satisfy
\[
\sum_{m\le k}w_{m,k}=\nu_k(S\ne\emptyset)\le1.
\]

First assume \(0<\theta<1\).  Split the maxima according to \(T_m\le \theta S_k/2\) and \(T_m>\theta S_k/2\).  On the first part, for all sufficiently large \(k\), the event \(|X|+1>\theta S_k\) implies \(|X|\ge (3/2)T_m\) when \(T_m>0\), and is even farther above the mean when \(T_m=0\).  A standard Chernoff bound for Poisson-binomial sums gives
\[
\mu_{m-1}(|X|+1>\theta S_k)\le \exp(-c_\theta S_k)
\]
with \(c_\theta>0\), uniformly in this part.  Hence the total small-\(T_m\) contribution is \(O(\exp(-c_\theta S_k))\).

On the second part, \(V_m\ge T_m/2>\theta S_k/4\).  The set
\[
\mathcal A_m\cap\{|X|+1>\theta S_k\}
\]
is still an antichain, so (1) gives
\[
\mu_{m-1}\bigl(\mathcal A_m\cap\{|X|+1>\theta S_k\}\bigr)
\le C_\theta S_k^{-1/2}.
\]
After summing against \(w_{m,k}\), the large-\(T_m\) contribution is \(O_\theta(S_k^{-1/2})\).  Since \(S_k\to\infty\), this proves the claim for \(0<\theta<1\).

It remains to treat \(\theta=0\).  Fix \(L>0\).  The indices with \(T_m\le L\) form a fixed finite initial segment independent of \(k\).  Their total max-fiber weight is bounded by a constant times
\[
\prod_{i>M_L}^{k}(1-q_i),
\]
where \(M_L\) is large enough that \(T_m\le L\) implies \(m\le M_L\).  This product tends to zero because \(\sum_i q_i=\infty\).  For \(T_m>L\), (1) gives
\[
\mu_{m-1}(\mathcal A_m)\le C(1+V_m)^{-1/2}\le C'(1+L)^{-1/2}.
\]
Taking \(k\to\infty\) and then \(L\to\infty\) proves
\[
\nu_k(\mathcal F_k)\to0,
\]
which is the \(\theta=0\) case.

This proves the proposition.

## Notes

This is not a proof of \(U_k(\theta)\to0\).  It kills the first concrete skeleton template, but arbitrary union-free families need not admit a max-fiber antichain decomposition of this kind.
