# Proposition: Robust corridor pairs have one relative overlap parity

## Statement

Use the common ancestor-signature setting of [[mrw-750fb7a7e30c]].  Let
\(i\) and \(j\) be two near-complete lower corridors with oriented sides
\[
S_i^0=U_i,\qquad S_i^1=W_i,\qquad
S_j^0=U_j,\qquad S_j^1=W_j,
\]
selected signatures \(\tau_i,\tau_j\), and side defects
\[
D_i^0=(1-\lambda_i)A_i,\qquad D_i^1=(1-\lambda_i)B_i,
\]
\[
D_j^0=(1-\lambda_j)A_j,\qquad D_j^1=(1-\lambda_j)B_j.
\]
For side bits \(b,c\in\{0,1\}\), write
\[
O_{bc}=w(S_i^b\cap S_j^c).
\]
Say that the side pair \((b,c)\) is robust when
\[
O_{bc}>D_i^b+D_j^c.
\tag{1}
\]

If some side pair \((b,c)\) is robust and has relative parity
\[
p=b+c\pmod 2,
\]
then the corridor signatures have relative parity \(p\):
\[
\tau_j=\operatorname{comp}^{p}(\tau_i).
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover every side pair of the opposite relative parity is defect-small:
\[
b'+c'\not\equiv p\pmod 2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
O_{b'c'}\le D_i^{b'}+D_j^{c'}.
\tag{3}
\]
Equivalently, with
\[
O^{(q)}_{ij}=\sum_{\substack{b,c\in\{0,1\}\\ b+c\equiv q\!\!\pmod2}}
w(S_i^b\cap S_j^c),
\]
one has
\[
O^{(1-p)}_{ij}
\le
D_i^0+D_i^1+D_j^0+D_j^1.
\tag{4}
\]

In the complete case \(\lambda_i=\lambda_j=1\), all defects vanish.  Hence if
any side overlap of relative parity \(p\) has positive weight, then every side
overlap of relative parity \(1-p\) has zero weight.  Thus two complete
corridors can share positive-weight vertices in at most one relative parity.

## Proof

Assume \((b,c)\) is robust and put \(p=b+c\pmod2\).  By
[[mrw-750fb7a7e30c]], robustness forces the selected pure side signatures to
agree:
\[
\tau_i^b=\tau_j^c.
\tag{5}
\]
Since \(\tau_i^0=\tau_i\), \(\tau_i^1=\bar\tau_i\), and similarly for \(j\),
equation (5) is exactly (2).

Now let \(b',c'\) have the opposite relative parity:
\[
b'+c'\not\equiv p\pmod2.
\]
Using (2),
\[
\tau_j^{c'}
=
\operatorname{comp}^{c'}(\tau_j)
=
\operatorname{comp}^{c'+p}(\tau_i).
\]
On the other hand
\[
\tau_i^{b'}=\operatorname{comp}^{b'}(\tau_i).
\]
The exponents \(b'\) and \(c'+p\) differ modulo \(2\), because
\(b'+c'\not\equiv p\).  Since the signature length satisfies \(m\ge1\), no
signature equals its coordinatewise complement.  Therefore
\[
\tau_i^{b'}\ne\tau_j^{c'}.
\]
Applying the side-overlap bound from [[mrw-206678825c7a]] gives (3).

There are exactly two side pairs with relative parity \(1-p\).  Summing the
two corresponding instances of (3) gives (4).  If
\(\lambda_i=\lambda_j=1\), then every \(D_i^b,D_j^c\) is zero, so (3) says
that every opposite-parity side overlap has weight zero.  A positive
same-parity side overlap is robust in this zero-defect case, giving the final
assertion.

## Notes

- This is the two-corridor local form of the signed-potential normal form.
  Once one relative parity is robust, the other relative parity cannot also
  carry substantial shared side mass except through the near-purity defects.
- Conceptually, this is the pairwise filter used inside the coherent
  component normal form [[mrw-a082a34f6797]], but its proof only needs the
  robust-edge implication and the incompatible-signature overlap bound.
- The result does not produce many corridors, force a robust odd-parity cycle,
  prove \(M_{P_k}(\theta)\to0\), prove \(U_k(\theta)\to0\), or lift to
  \(R_P(\theta)\).  It is a pairwise audit tool for coherent
  signature-potential assemblies.
