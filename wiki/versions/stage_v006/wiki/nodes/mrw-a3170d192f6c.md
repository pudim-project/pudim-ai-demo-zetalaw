# Corollary: Critical-point equation for the n=2 lower-envelope maximum

## Statement

For \(s>1\) and \(a>0\), put
\[
Z_s(a)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(a+k)^{-s}.
\]
For \(0<x<1\), define
\[
R(x)=\frac{P_2(x)}{C_2(x)}
=
\frac{2Z_3(x)Z_3(1/x)}
{3xZ_4(x)-Z_3(x)}
\]
and
\[
Q_2(x)=\frac{\log R(x)}{\log x}.
\]
Let
\[
\Lambda(x)=
-\frac{3Z_4(x)}{Z_3(x)}
+\frac{3Z_4(1/x)}{x^2Z_3(1/x)}
-\frac{6Z_4(x)-12xZ_5(x)}
{3xZ_4(x)-Z_3(x)}.
\]
Then there exists
\[
\xi\in\left(\frac{1409}{5000},\frac{293}{1000}\right)
\]
such that
\[
Q_2(\xi)>\frac{4629}{2000}
\]
and
\[
\xi\log \xi\,\Lambda(\xi)=\log R(\xi).
\]
Equivalently, \(Q_2'(\xi)=0\).

## Proof

By [[wiki/nodes/mrw-0241ab931d33|Even-order envelope reduction for polygamma beta windows]], the \(n=2\) functions
\[
C_2(x)=\psi''(x)+x\psi^{(3)}(x),
\qquad
P_2(x)=\psi''(x)\psi''(1/x)
\]
are positive on \(x>0\).  The standard polygamma expansion gives
\[
\psi''(x)=-2Z_3(x),
\qquad
\psi^{(3)}(x)=6Z_4(x),
\]
so
\[
C_2(x)=2(3xZ_4(x)-Z_3(x)),
\qquad
P_2(x)=4Z_3(x)Z_3(1/x),
\]
and hence the displayed formula for \(R(x)=P_2(x)/C_2(x)\).  These functions are smooth on \(0<x<1\), and \(R(x)>0\).

The logarithmic derivative of \(R\) is
\[
\frac{d}{dx}\log R(x)
=
\frac{d}{dx}\log Z_3(x)
+\frac{d}{dx}\log Z_3(1/x)
-\frac{d}{dx}\log(3xZ_4(x)-Z_3(x)).
\]
Since
\[
\frac{d}{dx}Z_3(x)=-3Z_4(x),
\qquad
\frac{d}{dx}Z_3(1/x)=\frac{3Z_4(1/x)}{x^2},
\]
and
\[
\frac{d}{dx}\bigl(3xZ_4(x)-Z_3(x)\bigr)
=6Z_4(x)-12xZ_5(x),
\]
the logarithmic derivative is exactly \(\Lambda(x)\).

Differentiating
\[
Q_2(x)=\frac{\log R(x)}{\log x}
\]
gives
\[
Q_2'(x)=
\frac{\Lambda(x)\log x-\log R(x)/x}{(\log x)^2}.
\]

By [[wiki/nodes/mrw-3712cf1c88d8|Refined compact localization for the n=2 lower-envelope maximum]],
\[
Q_2(1409/5000)<\frac{4629}{2000}<Q_2(23/80),
\]
and
\[
Q_2(293/1000)<\frac{4629}{2000}<Q_2(23/80).
\]
Therefore \(Q_2\) attains its maximum on
\[
\left[\,\frac{1409}{5000},\frac{293}{1000}\,\right]
\]
at an interior point \(\xi\), and \(Q_2(\xi)>4629/2000\).  Fermat's theorem gives \(Q_2'(\xi)=0\).  The derivative formula above is then equivalent to
\[
\xi\log \xi\,\Lambda(\xi)=\log R(\xi).
\]

## Notes

- This node converts the compact localization into a derivative target.  It does not prove uniqueness of the critical point or a global upper bound for \(Q_2\) on \((0,1)\).
- The next useful target is a sign certificate for \(x\log x\,\Lambda(x)-\log R(x)\) on rational subintervals of \([1409/5000,293/1000]\), together with an outside-bracket upper bound if needed.
