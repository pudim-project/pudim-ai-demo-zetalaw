# Note: Pair-slice obstruction for three-prime Erdos 536 weighted grids

## Statement

Let
\[
\Gamma_{235}(X)=
\{(i,j,k)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^3:2^i3^j5^k\le X\}.
\]
Let \(g_{235}(X)\) be the largest size of a subset of \(\Gamma_{235}(X)\) with no three distinct vectors whose pairwise coordinatewise maxima are all equal.  Applying the two-prime row-column theorem inside fixed \(5\)-adic slices proves
\[
g_{235}(X)\le
\#\{(i,j,k)\in\Gamma_{235}(X): i=0\text{ or }j=0\}.
\]
The resulting finite-prime weighted-grid bound is exactly
\[
\frac4{15}\int_1^\infty
\frac{\#\{(i,j,k)\in\Gamma_{235}(t): i=0\text{ or }j=0\}}{t^2}\,dt
=\frac56.
\]
Thus pair-slicing \(P=\{2,3,5\}\) along the best two-prime coordinates recovers the current two-prime \(5/6\) constant and cannot improve it.  A genuine three-prime improvement must use cross-slice constraints, in particular balanced lcm triangles such as
\[
(1,1,0),\qquad (1,0,1),\qquad (0,1,1),
\]
whose common coordinatewise maximum is not one of the selected points.

## Proof

Let \(S\subseteq\Gamma_{235}(X)\) contain no grid-bad triple.  For each \(k\ge0\), form the slice
\[
S_k=\{(i,j):(i,j,k)\in S\}.
\]
If \(S_k\) contained three distinct points in \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^2\) with equal pairwise coordinatewise maxima, then the corresponding three points in \(\Gamma_{235}(X)\) would be grid-bad because their third coordinate is the same \(k\).  Therefore the two-prime row-column theorem applies to every \(S_k\).

In the downset
\[
D_k(X)=\{(i,j):2^i3^j5^k\le X\},
\]
the row-column theorem bounds \(|S_k|\) by the number of points on the two coordinate axes:
\[
|S_k|\le
\#\{(i,j)\in D_k(X): i=0\text{ or }j=0\}.
\]
Summing over \(k\) gives
\[
|S|\le
\#\{(i,j,k)\in\Gamma_{235}(X): i=0\text{ or }j=0\}.
\]
Taking the maximum over \(S\) proves the displayed bound for \(g_{235}\).

Now use the jump-function identity from [[wiki/nodes/mrw-f835f9671070|Finite-prime weighted-grid reduction for Erdos 536]].  For any set \(E\) of exponent vectors,
\[
\int_1^\infty
\frac{\#\{\alpha\in E:\prod p^{\alpha_p}\le t\}}{t^2}\,dt
=
\sum_{\alpha\in E}\prod p^{-\alpha_p},
\]
provided the right side converges.  Here
\[
\sum_{\substack{i,j,k\ge0\\ i=0\text{ or }j=0}}2^{-i}3^{-j}5^{-k}
=
\left(\sum_{k\ge0}5^{-k}\right)
\left(
\sum_{i,j\ge0,\, i=0\text{ or }j=0}2^{-i}3^{-j}
\right).
\]
The two factors are
\[
\sum_{k\ge0}5^{-k}=\frac54
\]
and
\[
\sum_{i,j\ge0,\, i=0\text{ or }j=0}2^{-i}3^{-j}
=
\sum_{i\ge0}2^{-i}
+\sum_{j\ge0}3^{-j}
-1
=
2+\frac32-1=\frac52.
\]
Hence the integral equals
\[
\frac54\cdot\frac52=\frac{25}{8}.
\]
Since
\[
\delta_{235}=
\left(1-\frac12\right)
\left(1-\frac13\right)
\left(1-\frac15\right)
=\frac4{15},
\]
the induced density constant is
\[
\frac4{15}\cdot\frac{25}{8}=\frac56.
\]
This is exactly the two-prime row-column constant, not an improvement.

The obstruction is structural.  A fixed-\(k\) slice only sees bad triples whose common maximum lies inside that two-dimensional slice.  It does not use three-way balanced triples such as
\[
(1,1,0),\qquad (1,0,1),\qquad (0,1,1),
\]
for which each pairwise maximum is \((1,1,1)\) although no selected point is \((1,1,1)\).  Therefore any \(P=\{2,3,5\}\) route that is merely a sum of two-prime slice bounds leaves the \(5/6\) barrier unchanged.

## Notes

- Slicing along the other two-prime pairs is weaker: \((2,5)\)-slicing gives the constant \(9/10\), and \((3,5)\)-slicing gives the constant \(14/15\).
- This note does not prove that \(P=\{2,3,5\}\) cannot beat \(5/6\).  It only proves that the direct pair-slice transfer cannot do so.
