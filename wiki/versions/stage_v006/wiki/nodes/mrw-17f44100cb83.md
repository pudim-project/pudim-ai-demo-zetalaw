# Note: Tail-continuity obstruction for Erdos residue-class logarithmic density

## Statement

For Erdos Problem #25, finite residue shadows are not the hard part.  The finite approximants
\[
B_N=\{m:\ m<n_i\text{ or }m\not\equiv a_i\pmod {n_i}\text{ for }1\le i\le N\}
\]
have explicit logarithmic densities \(\delta_N\).  The core obstruction is continuity from above for the harmonic averages:
\[
B=\bigcap_{N=1}^{\infty}B_N.
\]
The unresolved positive-shadow case is therefore controlled by the tail sets
\[
E_N=B_N\setminus B
=
\{m\in B_N:\ \exists i>N,\ n_i\le m,\ m\equiv a_i\pmod {n_i}\}.
\]
The finite-shadow proposition proves that vanishing logarithmic upper density of \(E_N\) as \(N\to\infty\) is sufficient to solve the problem.

## Proof Audit

The reduction is local and follows from [[wiki/nodes/mrw-ba29cdf1fd30|Finite-shadow reduction for Erdos residue-class logarithmic density]]:
\[
\mu_x(B)=\mu_x(B_N)-\mu_x(E_N).
\]
Thus any proof that
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\limsup_{x\to\infty}\mu_x(E_N)=0
\]
immediately gives logarithmic density \(\delta=\lim_N\delta_N\) for \(B\).

The standard Davenport--Erdos multiples theorem is a benchmark rather than a proof here.  In the multiples case \(X_n=\{0\}\), divisibility supplies extra structure that is absent for arbitrary nonzero residue classes.  The next sprint must either recover a replacement for that structure or build an obstruction/counterexample candidate for the tail-continuity condition.

The local proposition [[wiki/nodes/mrw-f92c897044c4|Union-tail criterion for Erdos residue-class logarithmic density]] gives a first positive subcase:
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}
\limsup_{x\to\infty}
\frac1{\log x}
\sum_{\substack{i>N\\ n_i<x}}
\frac{1+\log(x/n_i)}{n_i}
=0
\]
is sufficient.  Its nested-multiples example shows this union criterion is not necessary; the full problem still requires an overlap-sensitive replacement.

The essential-index criterion [[wiki/nodes/mrw-171478aeed08|Essential-index tail criterion for Erdos residue-class logarithmic density]] gives a first such replacement: indices with zero finite-shadow density decrement remove no residual points and may be discarded before applying the tail majorant.  This captures fully redundant overlap but still does not handle partial redundancy among genuinely density-decreasing indices.

The block-uniform criterion [[wiki/nodes/mrw-e0778085804e|Block-uniform first-hit criterion for Erdos residue-class logarithmic density]] gives a conditional partial-redundancy mechanism.  For finite blocks \(G_r\) of first-hit sets, a uniform estimate of the form
\[
\sup_{x\ge2}\mu_x(G_r)\le C\eta_r+\varepsilon_r,\qquad \sum_r\varepsilon_r<\infty,
\]
implies the needed tail continuity.  The obstruction note [[wiki/nodes/mrw-536639208ce1|Escaping first-hit mass obstruction for finite-shadow decrement control]] shows why this cannot be replaced by the scalar decrement data \(h_i\) alone: fixed limiting densities do not control mass escaping along moving scales.

The CRT prefix-dispersion certificate [[wiki/nodes/mrw-2945dff32e3e|CRT prefix-dispersion certificate for Erdos first-hit block uniformity]] refines the block-uniform route.  For a finite first-hit block \(G_I\), it bounds \(\sup_x\mu_x(G_I)\) by the threshold-prefix concentration \(P_I\), the CRT residue-prefix dispersion \(R_I\), and an absolute multiple of the block decrement \(\eta_I\).  The remaining obstruction is therefore summability or failure of these finite certificate defects.
The small-residue obstruction [[wiki/nodes/mrw-e0971c9b820a|Small-residue obstruction to unshifted CRT prefix-defect summability]] proves that the unshifted \(R_I\) can fail for purely threshold-artifact reasons.  The preferred finite route is now the threshold-aware certificate [[wiki/nodes/mrw-f1348014e087|Threshold-aware CRT certificate for Erdos first-hit block uniformity]], which uses \(Q_I\) instead of \(R_I\).  The activation-scale bound [[wiki/nodes/mrw-eaf102b5cac0|Activation-scale bound for threshold-aware Erdos first-hit defects]] proves the sufficient estimate
\[
Q_I\le \frac{L_B}{n_B\log n_B}\eta_I,
\]
and the first-cycle entropy bound [[wiki/nodes/mrw-7586943cc138|First-cycle entropy bound for threshold-aware Erdos defects]] sharpens the support-size-only cost to \(Q_I\le\Phi(n_B,L_B\eta_I)\).  The remaining issue is whether residue structure improves this profile or whether actual first-hit mass can genuinely escape.

- The current finite-certificate frontier is not \(R_I\)-summability but improvement beyond the activation-scale \(Q_I\) ratio, or a residue-specific actual-mass obstruction.
