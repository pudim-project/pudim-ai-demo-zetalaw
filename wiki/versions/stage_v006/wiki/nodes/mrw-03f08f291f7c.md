# Proposition: Empty-bottom two-fiber assemblies reduce to shifted terminal residuals

## Statement
Let \(P=B\sqcup T\) be finite and fix a nonempty endpoint set \(u\subseteq B\).
For terminal families \(\mathcal R_0,\mathcal R_u\subseteq2^T\), define the
empty-bottom two-fiber assembly
\[
\mathcal F
=
\{R:R\in\mathcal R_0\}
\cup
\{u\cup R:R\in\mathcal R_u\}
\subseteq2^P.
\]
Throughout, pair-link-free means that there are no three pairwise distinct
members \(F_1,F_2,F_3\) with
\[
F_3\in I_P(F_1,F_2).
\]
For a terminal family \(\mathcal A\), set
\[
\mathsf J_T(\mathcal A)
=
\bigcup_{\substack{A,B\in\mathcal A\\A\ne B}}I_T(A,B).
\]

Then \(\mathcal F\) is pair-link-free if and only if all four conditions hold:

1. \(\mathcal R_0\) is pair-link-free in \(2^T\).

2. \(\mathcal R_u\) is pair-link-free in \(2^T\).

3. The lower fiber avoids the top-fiber two-point interval shadow:
   \[
   \mathcal R_0\cap\mathsf J_T(\mathcal R_u)=\emptyset.
   \]

4. Every mixed empty/top parent pair has no new top-fiber interval child: for
   every \(A\in\mathcal R_0\) and \(B\in\mathcal R_u\),
   \[
   \mathcal R_u\cap I_T(A,B)\subseteq\{B\}.
   \]

Consequently, if \(\nu_P=\pi_B\times\nu_T\) is a product law and
\[
\mathfrak M_T(a)
=
\sup\{\nu_T(\mathcal A\cap\{|R|>a\}):
\mathcal A\subseteq2^T,\ \mathcal A\text{ pair-link-free}\},
\]
then every pair-link-free two-fiber assembly of this form satisfies, for every
real \(L\),
\[
\nu_P(\mathcal F\cap\{|S|>L\})
\le
\pi_B(\emptyset)\mathfrak M_T(L)
+
\pi_B(u)\mathfrak M_T(L-|u|).
\]
The same bound applies to the contribution of the \(\emptyset\)- and \(u\)-fibers
inside any larger pair-link-free endpoint-fiber union, after restricting to
those two fibers.

## Proof
The endpoint intervals among the two available endpoint patterns are
\[
I_B(\emptyset,\emptyset)=\{\emptyset\},
\qquad
I_B(\emptyset,u)=I_B(u,\emptyset)=\{u\},
\qquad
I_B(u,u)=2^u.
\]
By the cross-pattern factorization `mrw-20ca89f696f2`, every pair-link triple in
\(\mathcal F\) must be one of the endpoint cases below.

First, if both parents and the child have endpoint \(\emptyset\), then the
ambient triple is exactly a terminal pair-link triple in \(\mathcal R_0\).
Thus such triples are absent exactly when \(\mathcal R_0\) is pair-link-free.

Second, if both parents and the child have endpoint \(u\), then the ambient
triple is exactly a terminal pair-link triple in \(\mathcal R_u\).  Thus these
triples are absent exactly when \(\mathcal R_u\) is pair-link-free.

Third, take two parents in the \(u\)-fiber and a child in the \(\emptyset\)-fiber.
The two top parents must be \(u\cup A\) and \(u\cup B\) with distinct
\(A,B\in\mathcal R_u\).  A lower child \(C\in\mathcal R_0\) forms a forbidden
triple exactly when
\[
C\in I_T(A,B).
\]
Because the child endpoint is \(\emptyset\), it is distinct from both top parents
even if \(C=A\) or \(C=B\).  Hence this endpoint case is excluded exactly by
\[
\mathcal R_0\cap\mathsf J_T(\mathcal R_u)=\emptyset.
\]

Fourth, take mixed parents \(A\in\mathcal R_0\) and \(u\cup B\) with
\(B\in\mathcal R_u\).  Since
\[
I_B(\emptyset,u)=\{u\},
\]
the child, if present in the two-fiber assembly, must lie in the top fiber and
has the form \(u\cup C\) with
\[
C\in\mathcal R_u\cap I_T(A,B).
\]
This child is automatically distinct from the lower parent \(A\), because
\(u\ne\emptyset\).  It coincides with the top parent exactly when \(C=B\).
Therefore the mixed endpoint case creates no forbidden pair-link triple exactly
when
\[
\mathcal R_u\cap I_T(A,B)\subseteq\{B\}
\]
for every \(A\in\mathcal R_0\) and \(B\in\mathcal R_u\).

There are no other endpoint cases: \(u\notin I_B(\emptyset,\emptyset)\), and
\(\emptyset\notin I_B(\emptyset,u)=I_B(u,\emptyset)\).  This proves both
necessity and sufficiency of the four conditions.

For the residual bound, the two endpoint pieces are disjoint, so
\[
\begin{aligned}
\nu_P(\mathcal F\cap\{|S|>L\})
&=
\pi_B(\emptyset)\nu_T(\mathcal R_0\cap\{|R|>L\})\\
&\quad+
\pi_B(u)\nu_T(\mathcal R_u\cap\{|R|>L-|u|\}).
\end{aligned}
\]
By the first two conditions, the two terminal fibers are pair-link-free.  The
definition of \(\mathfrak M_T\) gives
\[
\nu_T(\mathcal R_0\cap\{|R|>L\})
\le
\mathfrak M_T(L),
\]
and
\[
\nu_T(\mathcal R_u\cap\{|R|>L-|u|\})
\le
\mathfrak M_T(L-|u|).
\]
This proves the displayed bound.  If \(\mathcal F\) is part of a larger
pair-link-free endpoint-fiber union, the restriction to the two endpoint fibers
is pair-link-free by heredity, so the same argument applies.

## Notes
- Condition 4 removes only \(B\), not both terminal endpoints.  If \(C=A\), the
  top child \(u\cup A\) is still distinct from the lower parent \(A\), unless
  \(A=B\).
- The residual bound uses only the individual terminal pair-link-freeness of
  \(\mathcal R_0\) and \(\mathcal R_u\); the cross conditions can only further
  restrict the two-fiber branch.
- This is a residual quarantine, not terminal Erdos 536 evidence.  The shifted
  terminal windows \(\mathfrak M_T(L)\) and \(\mathfrak M_T(L-|u|)\) still need
  decay, absorption, or further structural input.
- Oracle accepted the proposition after adding the explicit pairwise-distinct
  convention for pair-link-free.
