# Proposition: Near-full corridors concentrate on one refined-signature pair

## Statement

Use the notation and hypotheses of [[mrw-a9efecc818c7]].  Thus
\(\mathcal F\subseteq2^P\) is pair-link-free, the first \(m\ge1\) nested
upper complete bipartite slice blocks select a coarse complementary corridor
\[
A=V_\tau,\qquad B=V_{\bar\tau},
\]
and the next \(\ell\ge1\) nested upper cuts refine the corridor into classes
\[
A_\omega,\qquad B_\omega
\qquad(\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}).
\]
Let \(w_v\ge0\) be weights on \(A\cup B\), and put
\[
\alpha_\omega=\sum_{a\in A_\omega}w_a,\qquad
\beta_\omega=\sum_{b\in B_\omega}w_b,
\]
\[
W_A=\sum_\omega\alpha_\omega,\qquad
W_B=\sum_\omega\beta_\omega.
\]
Assume \(W_AW_B>0\).  Define normalized refined-signature distributions
\[
p_\omega=\frac{\alpha_\omega}{W_A},\qquad
q_\omega=\frac{\beta_\omega}{W_B},
\]
and their complementary compatibility score
\[
\kappa(p,q)=\sum_{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}p_\omega q_{\bar\omega},
\qquad
\bar\omega=\mathbf 1-\omega.
\]
For the lower edge mass across the coarse corridor,
\[
M_Q(A,B)
=
\sum_{\substack{\{u,v\}\in E_Q^\mathcal F\\
|\{u,v\}\cap A|=|\{u,v\}\cap B|=1}}w_uw_v,
\]
one has
\[
M_Q(A,B)\le \kappa(p,q)W_AW_B.
\tag{1}
\]

Consequently, if \(1/2\le\lambda\le1\) and
\[
M_Q(A,B)\ge\lambda W_AW_B,
\tag{2}
\]
then there is a refined signature \(\omega_0\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}\) such that
\[
\alpha_{\omega_0}\ge\lambda W_A,
\qquad
\beta_{\bar\omega_0}\ge\lambda W_B.
\tag{3}
\]

In particular, if
\[
M_Q(A,B)=W_AW_B,
\tag{4}
\]
then there is a refined signature \(\omega_0\) such that
\[
\alpha_{\omega_0}=W_A,\qquad
\beta_{\bar\omega_0}=W_B,
\]
and every other refined class on both sides has zero weight.

## Proof

The corridor-refinement estimate [[mrw-a9efecc818c7]] gives
\[
M_Q(A,B)
\le
\sum_{\omega}\alpha_\omega\beta_{\bar\omega}.
\]
Since \(W_AW_B>0\), division by \(W_AW_B\) gives (1).

It remains to prove the near-equality rigidity statement.  Put
\[
r_\omega=q_{\bar\omega}.
\]
Then \(p=(p_\omega)\) and \(r=(r_\omega)\) are probability vectors on
\(\{0,1\}^{\ell}\), and
\[
\kappa(p,q)=\sum_\omega p_\omega r_\omega.
\]
From (1) and (2), \(\kappa(p,q)\ge\lambda\).

Let
\[
P=\max_\omega p_\omega.
\]
Because
\[
\sum_\omega p_\omega r_\omega\le \max_\omega p_\omega,
\]
we have \(P\ge\kappa(p,q)\ge\lambda\).

First suppose \(P>1/2\).  Choose \(\omega_0\) so that
\[
p_{\omega_0}=P.
\]
Write \(R=r_{\omega_0}\).  Since \(\sum_{\omega\ne\omega_0}r_\omega=1-R\),
\[
\kappa(p,q)
=
PR+\sum_{\omega\ne\omega_0}p_\omega r_\omega
\le
PR+(1-P)(1-R).
\tag{5}
\]
For fixed \(P\ge\lambda\ge1/2\), the right side of (5) is nondecreasing in
\(R\).  If \(R<\lambda\), then
\[
PR+(1-P)(1-R)
<
P\lambda+(1-P)(1-\lambda)
=
\lambda+(1-P)(1-2\lambda)
\le
\lambda,
\]
contradicting \(\kappa(p,q)\ge\lambda\).  Hence \(R\ge\lambda\).

Thus
\[
p_{\omega_0}\ge\lambda,
\qquad
q_{\bar\omega_0}=r_{\omega_0}\ge\lambda,
\]
which is exactly (3) after multiplying by \(W_A\) and \(W_B\).

It remains to handle the endpoint-tie case \(P=1/2\).  Then
\(\lambda=1/2\), since \(P\ge\lambda\ge1/2\).  Also
\[
\sum_\omega p_\omega r_\omega\le P=\frac12,
\]
so \(\kappa(p,q)\ge\lambda\) forces equality.  Equality in this bound means
that \(r\) is supported on the set
\[
S=\{\omega:p_\omega=1/2\}.
\]
The set \(S\) has at most two elements.  Choose \(\omega_0\in S\) with
\(r_{\omega_0}\) maximal.  Then \(p_{\omega_0}=1/2\) and
\[
r_{\omega_0}\ge\frac12.
\]
Therefore \(q_{\bar\omega_0}\ge1/2=\lambda\), and (3) follows in this final
case.

For the equality case, take \(\lambda=1\).  Then (3) gives
\[
\alpha_{\omega_0}\ge W_A,\qquad \beta_{\bar\omega_0}\ge W_B.
\]
Since the \(\alpha\)'s and \(\beta\)'s are nonnegative and sum to \(W_A\) and
\(W_B\), the displayed inequalities must be equalities, and all other refined
class weights are zero.

## Notes

- This is the near-equality case of the corridor-refinement bound, not a
  global anti-alignment exhaustion theorem.
- A near-complete lower corridor cannot be broadly distributed over many
  refined anti-corridors.  Once the normalized lower edge mass is at least
  \(\lambda\ge1/2\), one complementary refined-signature pair carries at least
  a \(\lambda\)-fraction of the weight on both coarse sides.
- The equality case says that a genuinely complete positive-weight corridor
  is refined-signature pure: every positive-weight vertex on one side has the
  same refined signature, and every positive-weight vertex on the other side
  has the complementary refined signature.
- The proposition does not prove that such heavy corridors occur globally, nor
  does it prove \(M_{P_k}(\theta)\to0\), \(U_k(\theta)\to0\), or an
  \(R_P(\theta)\) lift.  Its role is to classify the only way a heavy corridor
  can survive many added cuts.
