# Theorem: Erdos 536 intersecting complement defect shadow obstruction either gives fork or collapses to rank thin mass

## Statement

For every fixed \(0<\theta<1\) and \(\eta>0\), every family \(\mathcal F_k\subseteq H_{k,\theta}\) with \(\nu_k(\mathcal F_k)\ge\eta\) satisfies the following dichotomy for all large \(k\): either some occupied top \(C\in\mathcal F_k\) has a non-intersecting defect shadow \(\mathsf D_{\mathcal F_k}(C)\), hence a fork, or the intersecting-complement obstruction formed by the shadows \(\mathsf D_{\mathcal F_k}(C)\) coherently collapses \(\mathcal F_k\) into \(o(\sqrt{S_k})\) rank layers up to \(o(1)\) mass. Consequently every fork-free \(\mathcal F_k\subseteq H_{k,\theta}\) has \(\nu_k(\mathcal F_k)\to0\).
