# Theorem: Complete monotonicity of reciprocal-Gamma curvature

## Statement

Let
\[
H(x)=\log\Gamma(x)+\log\Gamma(1/x),\qquad x>0.
\]
Then \(H''\) is strictly completely monotonic on \((0,\infty)\).  Equivalently,
\[
(-1)^m\frac{d^m}{dx^m}H''(x)>0
\]
for every integer \(m\ge0\) and every \(x>0\).

More precisely,
\[
H''(x)=\int_0^\infty e^{-xt}K(t)\,dt,
\]
where
\[
K(t)=\frac{t}{e^t-1}
+t\sum_{n=1}^\infty\left(e^{-t/n}-1+\frac{t}{n}\right)
-\gamma t^2
\]
and \(K(t)>0\) for \(t>0\).

## Proof

The Weierstrass product gives, for \(x>0\),
\[
\log\Gamma(x)
=-\log x-\gamma x-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\left(\log\left(1+\frac{x}{n}\right)-\frac{x}{n}\right).
\]
After substituting \(1/x\), adding, and differentiating twice, the logarithmic second-derivative terms cancel and
\[
\begin{aligned}
H''(x)
&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{(x+n)^2}
-\frac{2\gamma}{x^3}\\
&\qquad
+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\left[
\frac1{(x+1/n)^2}
-\frac1{x^2}
+\frac{2}{nx^3}
\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
The differentiations are justified on compact subintervals of \((0,\infty)\) by uniform convergence after two derivatives.

Using
\[
\frac1{(x+a)^2}=\int_0^\infty t e^{-(x+a)t}\,dt,\qquad
\frac1{x^2}=\int_0^\infty t e^{-xt}\,dt,\qquad
\frac{2a}{x^3}=a\int_0^\infty t^2e^{-xt}\,dt,
\]
with \(a=1/n\), gives
\[
\frac1{(x+1/n)^2}-\frac1{x^2}+\frac{2}{nx^3}
=\int_0^\infty e^{-xt}t\left(e^{-t/n}-1+\frac{t}{n}\right)\,dt.
\]
Also
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{(x+n)^2}
=\int_0^\infty e^{-xt}\frac{t}{e^t-1}\,dt
\]
and
\[
-\frac{2\gamma}{x^3}
=\int_0^\infty e^{-xt}(-\gamma t^2)\,dt.
\]
Thus
\[
H''(x)=\int_0^\infty e^{-xt}K(t)\,dt
\]
with \(K\) as in the statement.

It remains to prove \(K(t)>0\).  Put
\[
\phi(u)=e^{-u}-1+u.
\]
Then \(\phi\ge0\) and \(\phi'=1-e^{-u}\ge0\).  Hence \(u\mapsto\phi(t/u)\) is positive and decreasing on \([1,\infty)\), so
\[
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi(t/n)\ge\int_1^\infty \phi(t/u)\,du.
\]
With \(v=t/u\),
\[
\int_1^\infty\phi(t/u)\,du
=t\int_0^t\frac{\phi(v)}{v^2}\,dv
=-\phi(t)+t\int_0^t\frac{1-e^{-v}}{v}\,dv.
\]
The standard identity
\[
\int_0^t\frac{1-e^{-v}}{v}\,dv
=\gamma+\log t+E_1(t),
\qquad
E_1(t)=\int_t^\infty\frac{e^{-v}}{v}\,dv
\]
therefore implies
\[
\frac{K(t)}{t}
\ge
\frac1{e^t-1}-\phi(t)+t\log t+tE_1(t).
\]
Since \(E_1(t)>0\) and \(\log t\ge1-1/t\) for \(t>0\),
\[
\frac{K(t)}{t}
>
\frac1{e^t-1}-\phi(t)+t-1
=\frac1{e^t-1}-e^{-t}
=\frac{e^{-t}}{e^t-1}>0.
\]
Thus \(K(t)>0\) for \(t>0\).  Differentiating under the Laplace integral gives
\[
(-1)^m\frac{d^m}{dx^m}H''(x)
=\int_0^\infty t^m e^{-xt}K(t)\,dt>0,
\]
which proves strict complete monotonicity.
